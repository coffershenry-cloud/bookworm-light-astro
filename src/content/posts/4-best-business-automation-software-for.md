  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 19px;">Understanding Zapier tasks</h3>
  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Zapier uses <b>tasks</b> to measure usage. A task is generally counted when an action successfully completes. Triggers do not count as tasks, and some built-in steps, such as filters and formatting, are excluded.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Consider a workflow that creates a CRM contact and sends a notification whenever a new lead arrives. If both actions complete successfully, they can use two tasks for that lead. If the workflow runs for 100 leads, that could mean approximately 200 tasks, assuming each action completes once and no other task-counting actions are involved.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">This is why the free plan's 100-task allowance is best treated as a testing allowance for simple workflows, not an unlimited automation solution.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Zapier also offers pay-per-task billing on eligible plans. If enabled, usage beyond the included allowance can generate additional charges. If it is disabled, workflows can pause when the task limit is reached.</p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 42px;">How to choose the right business automation platform</h2>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Comparing features and starting prices is useful, but the best platform is the one that fits the way your business operates. Before subscribing, consider these factors.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 19px;">1. Which applications do you need to connect?</h3>
  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Start by listing the applications your business already uses. These might include your CRM, accounting software, ecommerce platform, email marketing system, help desk and project management tool.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Then check whether each automation platform supports the exact applications and actions you need. An app appearing in an integration directory does not guarantee that every feature or action is supported.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 19px;">2. How complicated are your workflows?</h3>
  <p style="font-size: 16px;">A simple workflow might send a notification when a new form is submitted. A more advanced workflow might check the customer's account status, identify the correct department, update several records and request approval.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">For straightforward workflows, Zapier may be easier to manage. Make offers more visual control for branching processes. n8n is attractive when custom logic or code is important, while Power Automate is useful for Microsoft-centred processes.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 19px;">3. How does the platform charge for usage?</h3>
  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Do not compare automation tools by their starting prices alone. Their billing units differ:</p>

  <ul style="font-size: 16px;">
    <li><b>Zapier:</b> Successful actions generally count as tasks.</li>
    <li><b>Make:</b> Scenario actions consume credits, with some exceptions and advanced features using different amounts.</li>
    <li><b>n8n Cloud:</b> A complete workflow run counts as an execution, regardless of the number of steps.</li>
    <li><b>Power Automate:</b> Licensing is based on users, bots or hosted bots, depending on the plan.</li>
  </ul>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Estimate how many times a workflow will run each month and how many billable actions or steps it will perform. Include peak periods, retries and workflows that process multiple records.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 19px;">4. Who will build and maintain the workflows?</h3>
  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Automation does not remove the need for responsibility. Someone must configure workflows, monitor failures, update connections and check that the information being transferred is accurate.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">If nobody on your team has technical experience, a simpler managed platform may be more practical than self-hosting an advanced tool. If you have a developer or technically experienced employee, more flexible platforms may be worth considering.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 19px;">5. What happens when an automation fails?</h3>
  <p style="font-size: 16px;">A failed workflow can mean a missed customer enquiry, an incorrect invoice record or an order that never reaches the right employee. Before relying on automation, check how the platform handles errors, retries, logs and notifications.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">For important processes, build in safeguards. For example, do not automatically mark an invoice as paid simply because an automation received an unexpected response from another application.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 19px;">6. How will customer information be protected?</h3>
  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Automation platforms may process customer names, email addresses, payment-related records, order information and internal business data.</p>

  <p style="font-size: 16px;">Review the vendor's security documentation, access controls, data processing terms and available retention settings. Use only the permissions required for each connection, protect account credentials and avoid sending sensitive information to services that are not approved for that data.</p>
