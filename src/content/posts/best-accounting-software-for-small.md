<!--BUYING GUIDE-->
<h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(117, 70, 200); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(117, 70, 200); color: #202b3c; font-size: 27px; line-height: 1.35; margin: 48px 0px 15px; padding-bottom: 12px;">How to Choose the Right Accounting Software</h2>

<p style="font-size: 16px;">Comparing subscription prices is useful, but it should not be the only factor. The right platform should support the way your business earns money, pays expenses and maintains its financial records.</p>

<h3 style="color: #303846; font-size: 20px; margin-top: 25px;">1. Consider the size and structure of your business</h3>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">A sole proprietor with a small number of monthly transactions may not need the same system as a company with employees, multiple departments and a growing customer base.</p>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Identify who needs access to the accounts, how many transactions you process and whether you manage inventory, contractors or projects. This helps eliminate products that are either too limited or unnecessarily complicated.</p>

<h3 style="color: #303846; font-size: 20px; margin-top: 25px;">2. Check what each subscription includes</h3>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Two accounting plans can have similar monthly prices but very different restrictions. One may limit invoices, another may limit users, and another may charge extra for features such as payroll or additional team members.</p>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Before subscribing, check:</p>
<ul style="font-size: 16px; padding-left: 22px;">
<li>Number of users included.</li>
<li>Monthly or annual invoice limits.</li>
<li>Bank-feed availability and reconciliation tools.</li>
<li>Inventory and purchase-order features.</li>
<li>Receipt capture and expense management.</li>
<li>Payroll availability and additional charges.</li>
<li>Accountant access and integration options.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #303846; font-size: 20px; margin-top: 25px;">3. Make sure it supports your tax and recordkeeping needs</h3>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Accounting software can organise business transactions, but choosing a product does not automatically make your records complete or your tax filings correct.</p>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">The Internal Revenue Service explains that businesses may use an electronic accounting system or another recordkeeping method, provided it clearly records income and expenses and meets applicable recordkeeping requirements.</p>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Read the IRS guidance on <a href="https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #6841b7; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">what records a small business should keep</a> before deciding how to organise your financial information.</p>

<h3 style="color: #303846; font-size: 20px; margin-top: 25px;">4. Look at integrations before committing</h3>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Your accounting platform may need to work with payment processors, ecommerce stores, payroll services, customer-management software or inventory systems.</p>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Check whether the integrations you actually use are supported, whether they require an additional subscription and whether information synchronises automatically or needs manual handling.</p>

<h3 style="color: #303846; font-size: 20px; margin-top: 25px;">5. Calculate the long-term cost</h3>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">A discounted first few months can make a product appear less expensive than it will be over a full year. Compare the regular renewal price, optional add-ons, extra users and payment-processing charges.</p>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">A freelancer may find that an inexpensive invoicing plan becomes more costly after adding team members. A business with several employees may discover that a higher subscription tier is more practical than trying to manage everything through a limited entry-level plan.</p>

<!--UPGRADE-->
<h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(117, 70, 200); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(117, 70, 200); color: #202b3c; font-size: 27px; line-height: 1.35; margin: 48px 0px 15px; padding-bottom: 12px;">When Should a Small Business Upgrade Its Accounting Software?</h2>

<p style="font-size: 16px;">There is no universal revenue figure at which every business needs to upgrade. The decision depends on how much work your current system can handle and whether its limitations are creating unnecessary manual tasks.</p>

<p style="font-size: 16px;">Consider moving to a more capable plan or platform when:</p>
<ul style="font-size: 16px; padding-left: 22px;">
<li>You regularly reach invoice, transaction or user limits.</li>
<li>You need more detailed financial reports.</li>
<li>You have employees or contractors whose payments need to be tracked.</li>
<li>You begin managing inventory or more complicated purchasing activity.</li>
<li>You need multi-currency accounting.</li>
<li>Your accountant needs more direct access to your records.</li>
<li>Manual data entry is taking too much time or creating avoidable errors.</li>
</ul>
<p style="font-size: 16px;">Upgrading should solve a real business problem. Paying for advanced features your company does not use is not necessarily a sign of better financial management.</p>
