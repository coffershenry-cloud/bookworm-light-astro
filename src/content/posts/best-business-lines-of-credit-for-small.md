  <p style="color: #414650; font-size: 19px; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 22px;">
    Small businesses do not always receive customer payments when they need them. A supplier may require an upfront payment, an important invoice may be overdue, or seasonal demand may increase expenses before revenue arrives. A business line of credit can help manage these temporary cash flow gaps by giving a company access to funds when needed.&nbsp;</p><p style="color: #414650; font-size: 19px; line-height: 1.8; margin-bottom: 22px;"><img alt="Small business cash flow checklist" class="sFlh5c Jm0Uqe FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="427" src="/images/business-lines-of-credit-cash-flow.jpg" style="--xgflle: 1.499531396438613; max-width: 1600px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>
    Unlike a traditional business loan that provides a lump sum upfront, a revolving business line of credit generally allows a business to borrow up to an approved limit, repay what it uses and access available credit again. However, lenders differ significantly in their interest rates, fees, qualification requirements, repayment schedules and funding limits.
  </p>

  <p>
    This guide compares business lines of credit for small businesses in the United States, including online lenders and traditional banking options. It also explains how to evaluate the actual borrowing cost, since a low advertised starting rate does not necessarily mean a loan will be inexpensive for your business.
  </p>

  <h2 id="how-we-chose" style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(232, 234, 240); color: #253858; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 38px; padding-bottom: 10px;">How We Evaluated Business Line of Credit Providers</h2>

  <p>
    A business line of credit is a financial commitment. A provider should not be selected simply because it advertises fast funding or a large maximum credit limit. The real value depends on how much the business can borrow, how much it will cost and whether the repayment structure matches its cash flow.
  </p>

  <p>We considered the following factors:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 23px;">
    <li><b>Credit limits:</b> The maximum financing amount advertised by the provider.</li>
    <li><b>Borrower eligibility:</b> Personal credit, business revenue and time-in-business requirements.</li>
    <li><b>Borrowing cost:</b> Interest rates, APRs, monthly fees, origination fees and other charges.</li>
    <li><b>Repayment structure:</b> Weekly or monthly payments, repayment periods and how available credit is restored.</li>
    <li><b>Funding process:</b> Application requirements and how quickly approved funds may become available.</li>
    <li><b>Transparency:</b> Whether the lender explains its costs, fees and important loan conditions.</li>
    <li><b>Business suitability:</b> Whether the product fits common small business working-capital needs.</li>
  </ul>

  <div style="background: rgb(255, 248, 233); border-left: 5px solid rgb(226, 164, 59); border-radius: 7px; margin: 24px 0px; padding: 17px 20px;">
    <b>Our approach:</b> This is an editorial comparison of available products and published terms. It is not a claim that every lender has been independently tested or that every business will qualify. A lender's advertised minimum requirements do not guarantee approval.
  </div>

  <h2 id="amex" style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(232, 234, 240); color: #253858; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 10px;">4. American Express Business Line of Credit: Best for Installment-Based Draws</h2>

  <p>
    <b>American Express Business Line of Credit</b> offers eligible businesses access to credit lines ranging from $2,000 to $250,000. It is important to understand how this product works because it does not operate exactly like a conventional revolving credit account.
  </p>

  <p>
    Each draw creates a separate installment loan with its own repayment schedule and monthly loan fee. This can make the amount and schedule of each borrowing transaction easier to identify, but it also means that multiple draws can create multiple repayment obligations.
  </p>

  <div style="background: rgb(245, 247, 251); border-radius: 8px; border: 1px solid rgb(224, 229, 238); margin: 20px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #253858; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 12px;">American Express at a Glance</h3>
    <ul style="margin: 0px; padding-left: 22px;">
      <li><b>Credit line:</b> $2,000 to $250,000 for eligible customers.</li>
      <li><b>Minimum FICO score:</b> 660, although the required score may be higher.</li>
      <li><b>Time in business:</b> At least 12 months.</li>
      <li><b>Monthly revenue:</b> At least $3,000 in recent average monthly revenue.</li>
      <li><b>Loan terms:</b> Eligible draws may have six-, 12-, 18- or 24-month installment terms.</li>
      <li><b>Loan fees:</b> Published monthly fee range of 0.55% to 1.55% of the original principal amount.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">Understanding the Monthly Loan Fee</h3>

  <p>
    American Express charges a monthly loan fee rather than a conventional interest rate on this product. The fee is calculated as a percentage of the original principal and is charged for each month the installment loan remains outstanding.
  </p>

  <p>
    The fee percentage varies by applicant and loan terms. The company states that the available loan fee and a comparable APR are presented in the loan agreement. This makes it important to review the APR comparison and total repayment amount before accepting a draw.
  </p>

  <p>
    The product also requires a personal guarantee and is secured by business assets. These conditions matter because the borrower may have personal and business financial exposure if the business cannot repay.
  </p>

  <h2 id="how-it-works" style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(232, 234, 240); color: #253858; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 10px;">How Does a Business Line of Credit Work?</h2>

  <p>
    A business line of credit is a financing arrangement that gives an approved business access to a set amount of credit. Instead of receiving the entire amount at once, the business can request funds when necessary, subject to the agreement.
  </p>

  <h2 id="costs" style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(232, 234, 240); color: #253858; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 10px;">Business Line of Credit Rates, Fees and Borrowing Costs</h2>

  <p>
    The cost of a business line of credit depends on the lender, the amount borrowed, the repayment period and the financial strength of the business. A lender may advertise an attractive starting rate, but the actual offer can be considerably higher.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">1. Interest Rate and APR</h3>

  <p>
    The interest rate is the percentage charged for borrowing money. The annual percentage rate, or APR, is designed to represent the annualized cost of borrowing and can include certain fees as well as interest.
  </p>

  <p>
    APR is especially useful when comparing products with different fee structures. However, check how each lender calculates and discloses its APR, particularly when the product uses fixed fees, factor rates or installment-based draws.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">2. Origination Fees</h3>

  <p>
    Some lenders charge an origination fee when a credit facility or individual draw is created. This may be deducted from the amount received or added to the repayment obligation.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">3. Maintenance or Annual Fees</h3>

  <p>
    A lender may charge a fee for keeping a credit line open, even when the business does not borrow money. Check whether the account has an annual fee, monthly maintenance fee or inactivity charge.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">4. Draw and Transaction Fees</h3>

  <p>
    Some providers charge fees when funds are withdrawn or transferred. The amount may depend on the withdrawal method, transaction size or product structure.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">5. Late Payment and Returned Payment Fees</h3>

  <p>
    Missing a repayment can result in additional charges. A returned payment may also attract a separate fee. Read the agreement carefully and make sure the payment schedule fits your expected cash flow.
  </p>

  <div style="background: rgb(243, 240, 255); border-radius: 8px; border: 1px solid rgb(217, 205, 245); margin: 26px 0px; padding: 19px 21px;">
    <h3 style="color: #5932a4; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">Do Not Compare Only the Starting Rate</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      Ask each lender for the amount you will receive, the total amount you must repay, the APR, all fees, the payment frequency and the consequences of early repayment. These details provide a more useful comparison than an advertised starting rate alone.
    </p>
  </div>

  <h2 id="requirements" style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(232, 234, 240); color: #253858; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 10px;">Business Line of Credit Requirements: What Do Lenders Look For?</h2>

  <p>
    Lenders evaluate the financial condition of a business before approving a line of credit. Requirements differ, but most applications consider several common factors.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">Personal and Business Credit History</h3>

  <p>
    A lender may review the owner's personal credit history, the business's commercial credit profile or both. A stronger credit history can improve the chances of qualifying for more favorable terms, although approval also depends on revenue and other financial factors.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">Annual or Monthly Revenue</h3>

  <p>
    Revenue helps lenders understand whether a business generates enough money to support repayment. Some providers publish minimum monthly or annual revenue requirements, while others assess bank statements and cash flow during underwriting.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">Time in Business</h3>

  <p>
    Established businesses may have a longer operating history and more financial information for lenders to review. Some online lenders consider relatively young businesses, while traditional banks may have more demanding eligibility standards.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">Business Bank Statements</h3>

  <p>
    Lenders may request recent business bank statements to evaluate deposits, account balances, cash flow and existing financial obligations. Some online providers use connected bank accounts to review this information.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">Business Financial Statements</h3>

  <p>
    Depending on the lender and credit limit, applicants may need to provide a profit and loss statement, balance sheet, tax returns or other financial records.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">Personal Guarantee and Collateral</h3>

  <p>
    Some business credit lines require a personal guarantee, which can make the owner personally responsible if the business defaults. Secured credit lines may also require the business to pledge assets.
  </p>

  <h2 id="choosing" style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(232, 234, 240); color: #253858; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 10px;">How to Choose the Right Business Line of Credit</h2>

  <p>
    The right lender depends on the financial condition of your business and the reason you need credit. Use these steps to narrow down your options.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">1. Work Out How Much You Actually Need</h3>

  <p>
    Estimate the size of the cash flow gap or expense you want to cover. Avoid borrowing a larger amount simply because a lender offers a high credit limit. A larger facility can create unnecessary borrowing temptation and may come with additional costs.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">2. Compare Your Eligibility</h3>

  <p>
    Check the lender's minimum credit score, time-in-business requirement and revenue criteria. Applying to lenders whose requirements are far beyond your current financial profile may waste time and could result in unnecessary credit inquiries.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">3. Compare APR and Total Repayment</h3>

  <p>
    Request a full breakdown of the borrowing cost. Compare the APR, fees, repayment frequency and total amount payable. Be particularly careful with products that use fixed fees or factor rates rather than a conventional interest rate.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">4. Check Whether the Credit Is Truly Revolving</h3>

  <p>
    Do not assume that every product called a line of credit lets you borrow and repay repeatedly under the same structure. Some providers treat each draw as a separate installment loan. Make sure you understand when credit becomes available again and whether the lender can reduce or suspend the facility.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">5. Review Personal Guarantees and Collateral</h3>

  <p>
    Read the agreement to determine whether you are personally guaranteeing repayment or pledging business assets. Understand what could happen if the business misses payments or defaults.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 20px;">6. Check the Repayment Schedule Against Your Cash Flow</h3>

  <p>
    A weekly repayment schedule may be manageable for a business with frequent incoming payments, but it could create pressure for a company that receives most of its revenue monthly or seasonally.
  </p>

  <p>
    Choose a repayment schedule that the business can sustain without relying on another loan to cover the payments.
  </p>

  <h2 id="faqs" style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(232, 234, 240); color: #253858; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 10px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">1. What is the best business line of credit for a small business?</h3>
  <p>
    The best option depends on the company's revenue, credit history, operating history and funding needs. Bluevine is worth comparing for online working capital, Wells Fargo for traditional bank financing, Fundbox for certain younger businesses and American Express for installment-based draws. Compare actual offers before choosing.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">2. How much can a small business borrow through a line of credit?</h3>
  <p>
    Credit limits vary by lender and borrower. The providers in this guide advertise limits ranging from smaller amounts to $250,000. Some SBA-supported programs can provide larger revolving facilities for eligible businesses. The amount a business receives depends on underwriting and the terms of the particular program.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">3. What credit score do I need for a business line of credit?</h3>
  <p>
    There is no universal minimum. Some online lenders consider applicants with personal credit scores around 600, while other products may require a score of 625, 660 or higher. Traditional banks may evaluate the full financial profile rather than relying on one published minimum.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">4. Can a new business get a line of credit?</h3>
  <p>
    It may be possible, but newer businesses often have fewer financing options because they have limited operating history and financial records. Some online lenders consider businesses that have operated for only a few months, subject to revenue and credit requirements.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">5. Is a business line of credit better than a business credit card?</h3>
  <p>
    Not necessarily. A business line of credit may provide access to larger amounts of working capital, while a business credit card can be convenient for everyday purchases and may offer rewards. Compare interest, fees, repayment terms, credit limits and how each product fits the business's spending needs.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">6. Do business lines of credit require collateral?</h3>
  <p>
    Some are unsecured, while others are secured by business assets. A lender may also require a personal guarantee. Review the agreement carefully because an unsecured line of credit can still involve personal liability if a personal guarantee is required.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">7. Does a business line of credit affect personal credit?</h3>
  <p>
    It can, depending on the lender's application process, reporting practices and whether the owner provides a personal guarantee. Some lenders may check personal credit during an application, and missed payments or defaults may have personal consequences under the agreement. Ask the lender how it reports account activity before applying.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">8. How quickly can a business line of credit be approved?</h3>
  <p>
    Some online lenders advertise decisions within hours or a short period after receiving an application. Traditional bank applications can take longer, especially when additional documentation is needed. Approval and funding times are not guaranteed.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #37445c; font-size: 19px;">9. Can I use a business line of credit to pay operating expenses?</h3>
  <p>
    Many business lines of credit are designed for working capital, including inventory, supplier payments and other short-term operating expenses. However, permitted uses depend on the lender and loan agreement. Confirm that the intended expense is allowed before drawing funds.
  </p>

  <p>
    A business line of credit can help a small business manage cash flow, cover temporary expenses and respond to opportunities without taking out a new term loan every time money is needed. However, the convenience of flexible access should not distract from the cost of borrowing.
