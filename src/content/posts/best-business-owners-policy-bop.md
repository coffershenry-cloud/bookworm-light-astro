<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">What Does a Business Owners Policy Cover?</h2>

<p>A BOP is designed to package several important forms of small-business insurance. The exact coverage depends on the insurer and policy, but three major protection areas are commonly associated with BOP insurance.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">1. General liability insurance</h3>

<p>This portion helps protect your business against certain third-party claims involving bodily injury, property damage and personal or advertising injury.</p>

<p>For example, imagine a customer visits your store, slips on a wet floor and suffers an injury. If the customer makes a claim against your business, the general liability portion of an applicable BOP may help cover eligible legal defence costs, settlements or judgments.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">2. Commercial property insurance</h3>

<p>Commercial property coverage helps protect eligible physical business assets against covered losses.</p>

<p>Depending on your policy, this may include:</p>

<ul>
<li>Buildings you own or have insured responsibility for.</li>
<li>Office furniture and fixtures.</li>
<li>Computers and business equipment.</li>
<li>Tools and machinery.</li>
<li>Inventory and stock.</li>
<li>Other eligible business personal property.</li>
</ul>

<p>For example, a fire damages the equipment and inventory inside your shop. Commercial property coverage may help pay for repair or replacement costs, subject to the policy's covered causes of loss, limits and deductible.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">3. Business income and interruption coverage</h3>

<p>Business income coverage may help replace lost income and certain continuing operating expenses when a covered property loss forces your business to suspend or reduce operations.</p>

<p>Imagine a storm damages your insured premises and forces your business to close temporarily while repairs are completed. If the loss meets the policy's requirements, business income coverage may help with eligible lost income and covered expenses during the recovery period.</p>

<p>Some policies also offer extra expense protection, which can help with certain additional costs incurred to keep the business operating or resume operations sooner.</p>

<div style="background: rgb(255, 245, 232); border-left: 5px solid rgb(214, 138, 41); border-radius: 8px; margin: 22px 0px; padding: 16px 18px;">
<p style="margin: 0px;"><b>Important:</b> Business interruption coverage is not automatic protection against every reason a business loses income. It commonly requires a qualifying covered physical loss, and waiting periods, restoration periods, exclusions and limits apply.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">What Does a BOP Usually Not Cover?</h2>

<p>A BOP can provide a useful foundation, but it does not replace every type of commercial insurance.</p>

<p>Depending on the insurer and policy, the following protections may need to be purchased separately or added through an endorsement:</p>

<ul>
<li><b>Workers' compensation:</b> Helps address eligible employee work-related injuries and illnesses. Most states require it for businesses with employees, subject to state-specific rules and exceptions.</li>
<li><b>Commercial auto:</b> Protects against covered risks involving vehicles used for business purposes.</li>
<li><b>Professional liability:</b> Also called errors and omissions insurance, this helps address claims involving professional mistakes, advice or services.</li>
<li><b>Cyber liability:</b> Helps address certain costs associated with data breaches, ransomware and other cyber incidents.</li>
<li><b>Flood insurance:</b> Standard commercial property coverage commonly excludes flood damage, so separate coverage may be needed.</li>
<li><b>Employment practices liability:</b> Can help address certain employee-related claims, such as discrimination or wrongful termination, subject to policy terms.</li>
<li><b>Commercial umbrella insurance:</b> Can provide additional liability limits above certain underlying policies.</li>
</ul>

<p>For example, a consulting firm may need professional liability insurance because its main exposure comes from advice or services. A retail business with employees may need workers' compensation, while a company that uses vehicles for deliveries may need commercial auto insurance.</p>

<p>Do not assume that a BOP covers these risks just because it is described as a comprehensive business insurance package. Ask the insurer which protections are included, optional or excluded.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">Who Should Consider a Business Owners Policy?</h2>

<p>A BOP can be particularly useful for small businesses that have both liability exposure and physical assets to protect.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Retail stores</h3>
<p>Retail businesses may have customer foot traffic, inventory, shelving, display equipment and other property that needs protection. A BOP can help address both certain customer liability claims and covered damage to business property.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Restaurants and food businesses</h3>
<p>Restaurants have equipment, furniture, food inventory and customer-facing premises. Owners may want to investigate additional coverage for food spoilage, equipment breakdown, liquor liability and other industry-specific risks.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Professional offices</h3>
<p>Consultants, accountants, marketing agencies and other professional service businesses may have office equipment, furniture and client-facing premises. A BOP can help address certain property and general liability risks, while professional liability may be needed for errors in their services.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Contractors and trades businesses</h3>
<p>Contractors may have offices, tools, machinery and other business property to protect. They should also investigate whether they need separate tools and equipment coverage, commercial auto, workers' compensation or additional liability limits.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Home-based businesses</h3>
<p>Some eligible home-based businesses may qualify for a BOP. However, standard homeowners insurance may not adequately protect business equipment, inventory or commercial liability exposures. Confirm how the policy treats business property located at home and property taken off premises.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">How to Choose the Right BOP Insurance for Your Business</h2>

<p>Choosing a policy should involve more than comparing monthly premiums. Use the following steps to narrow down your options.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">1. Identify your business risks</h3>
<p>Make a list of the physical assets you need to protect, the people who visit your premises, the services you provide and the events that could interrupt your operations.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">2. Calculate your property value</h3>
<p>Estimate the replacement cost of your equipment, furniture, inventory and other insured property. Avoid choosing limits based only on the original purchase price if replacement costs have changed.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">3. Compare liability limits</h3>
<p>Review the per-occurrence and aggregate limits. Consider your industry, customer exposure, contracts and the potential cost of a serious claim.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">4. Review business income protection</h3>
<p>Ask how long the policy may cover lost income after a covered property loss, whether there is a waiting period, and which operating expenses qualify.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">5. Examine deductibles and exclusions</h3>
<p>Understand what you must pay before coverage applies and which causes of loss are excluded. Pay particular attention to flood, earthquake, equipment breakdown and other risks relevant to your location and operations.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">6. Ask about optional coverage</h3>
<p>Depending on your business, you may need cyber, professional liability, employment practices liability, hired and non-owned auto, equipment breakdown or other enhancements.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">7. Compare the actual insurers and policy documents</h3>
<p>If you purchase through an agent or marketplace, identify the company underwriting the policy. Review the declarations, coverage forms, endorsements and exclusions before accepting the quote.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">8. Review your policy every year</h3>
<p>Your business may grow, acquire new equipment, hire employees or move to a different location. Review your insurance limits and coverage needs at renewal so your policy reflects your current operations.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">What is the best BOP insurance company for small businesses?</h3>
<p>There is no single best provider for every business. The Hartford performed strongly in MoneyGeek's September 2026 pricing and provider comparison. Chubb is worth considering for broader customization and larger eligible businesses, while ERGO NEXT offers an online-first purchasing experience. The best choice depends on your industry, location, property, coverage limits and service preferences.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">How much does BOP insurance cost per month?</h3>
<p>MoneyGeek reported an average monthly BOP rate of approximately $147 in its September 2026 study. The Hartford averaged $115, ERGO NEXT $154 and Nationwide $146 in the provider data presented. These are study benchmarks, not guaranteed quotes. Your actual premium depends on your business profile and coverage selections.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Is a BOP required by law?</h3>
<p>A BOP is not generally required by one nationwide law for every small business. However, a landlord, lender, client or commercial contract may require certain insurance protections. Separate policies, such as workers' compensation or commercial auto, may be legally required depending on your business and state.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Does a BOP include business interruption insurance?</h3>
<p>Many BOP policies include business income or interruption coverage, but the exact terms vary. It generally applies when a covered property loss causes a qualifying interruption. Review the waiting period, restoration period, limits and exclusions.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Does a BOP cover employees?</h3>
<p>A BOP does not generally replace workers' compensation insurance. If your employees suffer eligible work-related injuries or illnesses, workers' compensation may be required under your state's rules. A BOP may also offer optional employment practices liability coverage, which addresses a different category of risk.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Can a home-based business get a BOP?</h3>
<p>Some insurers offer BOP coverage to eligible home-based businesses. The policy may help protect business property and certain liability exposures, but eligibility and coverage for property at home or away from the premises vary. Confirm that your specific business activities and property are covered.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Does a BOP cover professional mistakes?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. Professional liability or errors and omissions insurance is generally a separate coverage. Some insurers may offer it as an endorsement or additional option for eligible businesses, but a standard BOP should not be assumed to cover professional negligence claims.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Can I buy a BOP online?</h3>
<p>Some providers, including ERGO NEXT, offer online quotes and purchasing for eligible businesses. Other insurers may require an agent or broker to help assess your business risks and arrange the policy.</p>
