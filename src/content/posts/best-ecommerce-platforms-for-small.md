<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">1. Shopify — Best Overall Ecommerce Platform for Small Businesses</h2>

<p><b>Shopify</b> is a dedicated ecommerce platform designed to help businesses build and operate online stores without managing their own web hosting or server infrastructure.</p>

<p>It is a strong option for entrepreneurs who want to focus on selling rather than assembling different tools to make a store work.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Key Shopify features</h3>

<ul>
<li><b>Online store builder:</b> Create a storefront using customizable themes and built-in editing tools.</li>
<li><b>Product management:</b> Add products, organize collections, manage variants and track inventory.</li>
<li><b>Payment processing:</b> Accept payments through Shopify Payments where available, or connect supported third-party payment providers.</li>
<li><b>Multichannel selling:</b> Connect your store with supported social platforms, marketplaces and in-person sales channels.</li>
<li><b>Order management:</b> Track orders, manage fulfillment and handle customer information from one dashboard.</li>
<li><b>App ecosystem:</b> Add marketing, shipping, accounting, customer service and other capabilities through third-party apps.</li>
<li><b>Built-in business tools:</b> Access analytics, marketing features and AI-powered tools such as Shopify Magic and Sidekick.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Shopify pricing</h3>

<p>Shopify offers several plans. Its Basic plan starts at $29 per month when billed annually, while the Grow plan costs $79 per month and Advanced costs $299 per month on annual billing.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">What we like about Shopify</h3>

<ul>

<li>It supports both online and in-person selling through its broader commerce ecosystem.</li>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">2. WooCommerce — Best for WordPress Users Who Want Flexibility</h2>

<p><b>WooCommerce</b> is an open-source ecommerce platform that operates as a plugin for WordPress.</p><p><img alt="WooCommerce Logo (Woo Logo), symbol, meaning, history, PNG, brand" class="sFlh5c Jm0Uqe FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="360" src="https://logos-world.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/WooCommerce-Logo.jpg" style="--xgflle: 1.7777777777777777; max-width: 3840px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

<p>Unlike Shopify and BigCommerce, WooCommerce does not charge a standard monthly subscription for its core ecommerce software. Instead, you install it on a WordPress website and choose your own hosting provider, themes, extensions and payment solutions.</p>

<p>This gives businesses considerable flexibility, but it also means they take greater responsibility for managing the website.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Key WooCommerce features</h3>

<ul>
<li><b>Open-source software:</b> Access and customize the core platform without a recurring WooCommerce subscription.</li>
<li><b>Product management:</b> Sell physical products, digital downloads, variations and other supported product types.</li>
<li><b>Payment flexibility:</b> Connect supported payment gateways, including WooPayments and other third-party providers.</li>
<li><b>WordPress integration:</b> Combine ecommerce with blogging, content publishing and other WordPress capabilities.</li>
<li><b>Extensions:</b> Add subscriptions, bookings, shipping integrations, marketing tools and additional store functionality.</li>
<li><b>Customization:</b> Modify the storefront and its functionality through themes, extensions and custom development.</li>
<li><b>Store ownership:</b> You control the WordPress installation, hosting arrangement and many aspects of the website infrastructure.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">WooCommerce pricing</h3>

<p>The WooCommerce core plugin is free to download and use. However, that does not mean operating a complete online store costs nothing.</p>

<p>WooCommerce estimates hosting costs of approximately $25 to $350 per month for most stores, depending on their requirements. Paid extensions can cost approximately $29 to $299 per year each.</p>

<p>Your total cost may also include:</p>

<ul>
<li>Domain registration.</li>
<li>Premium WordPress themes.</li>
<li>Paid plugins and extensions.</li>
<li>Payment processing fees.</li>
<li>Website maintenance and security services.</li>
<li>Developer assistance, if needed.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">What we like about WooCommerce</h3>

<ul>
<li>The core platform does not charge a monthly subscription or take a share of store revenue.</li>
<li>It offers extensive customization possibilities.</li>
<li>It works particularly well for businesses already using WordPress.</li>
<li>Businesses can choose their own hosting and payment providers.</li>
<li>Its extension ecosystem supports many different ecommerce requirements.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Where WooCommerce falls short</h3>

<ul>
<li>You are responsible for choosing and maintaining hosting.</li>
<li>Updates, backups, security and performance require attention.</li>
<li>Too many poorly maintained plugins can create compatibility or performance problems.</li>
<li>Some advanced store features require paid extensions.</li>
<li>Businesses without technical support may find setup and maintenance more demanding than with a hosted platform.</li>
</ul>

<div style="background: rgb(243, 240, 255); border-radius: 8px; margin: 20px 0px; padding: 16px 18px;">
<p style="margin: 0px;"><b>Best fit:</b> Businesses already using WordPress, or those that want greater control over their store and are comfortable managing hosting, plugins and maintenance.</p>
</div>

<p><b>BigCommerce</b> is a hosted ecommerce platform designed for businesses that want substantial commerce functionality without assembling every feature through separate applications.</p><p><img alt="Press Center | BigCommerce" class="sFlh5c Jm0Uqe FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="234" src="https://storage.googleapis.com/s.mkswft.com/RmlsZTo3NzQyZWFmYy1iMTY5LTQxNzItYTcxNi1iNWRjNzA1YWRjMDA=/bg-image.webp" style="--xgflle: 2.72972972972973; max-width: 808px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

<p>It is worth considering when a store is growing beyond a simple catalog and needs more sophisticated product management, sales channels, integrations or operational features.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Key BigCommerce features</h3>

<ul>
<li><b>Storefront builder:</b> Build and manage an online store using customizable themes and editing tools.</li>
<li><b>Product catalog management:</b> Organize products, variants and inventory.</li>
<li><b>Multichannel selling:</b> Connect supported marketplaces and social commerce channels.</li>
<li><b>Built-in commerce features:</b> Access features such as discounts, promotions, customer groups and other selling tools, depending on the plan.</li>
<li><b>Payment integrations:</b> Connect supported payment providers and gateways.</li>
<li><b>Multi-Storefront:</b> Higher-tier and eligible arrangements support multiple storefronts from one account.</li>
<li><b>Business integrations:</b> Connect external systems for shipping, marketing, accounting and other operations.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">BigCommerce pricing</h3>

<p>BigCommerce's current annual-billing prices begin at $29 per month for Core. Growth costs $79 per month, while Scale costs $299 per month.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">4. Wix — Best for Design-Focused Small Online Stores</h2>

<p><b>Wix</b> combines website building with ecommerce functionality. It is particularly appealing to business owners who want control over the appearance of their website without having to work with code.</p>

<p>Rather than focusing exclusively on online selling, Wix gives businesses a broader website-building environment that includes ecommerce, marketing, bookings and other business tools.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Key Wix ecommerce features</h3>

<ul>
<li><b>Drag-and-drop editor:</b> Customize pages and layouts using a visual website editor.</li>
<li><b>Store management:</b> Add products, organize collections and manage orders.</li>
<li><b>Payment acceptance:</b> Connect supported payment methods through eligible business plans.</li>
<li><b>Inventory tools:</b> Manage product stock and product variations.</li>
<li><b>Abandoned cart recovery:</b> Encourage customers who leave without completing checkout to return.</li>
<li><b>Marketing tools:</b> Access email marketing, promotions, social selling and other business features depending on the plan.</li>
<li><b>Bookings and services:</b> Combine product sales with appointments, bookings and service offerings.</li>
<li><b>AI website tools:</b> Use Wix's AI-assisted tools to help create and manage website content.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Wix pricing</h3>

<p>Wix offers a free website plan, but the free plan is not suitable for launching a fully functioning ecommerce store with payment acceptance. Businesses generally need an eligible paid plan.</p>

<p>Its U.S. reference pricing for annual billing places Core at around $29 per month, Business at around $39 per month and Business Elite at around $159 per month. Prices can vary by location, taxes and billing arrangement.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">5. Squarespace — Best for Creative Brands and Visually Appealing Stores</h2>

<p><b>Squarespace</b> is known for its professionally designed templates and visual website-building experience. Its ecommerce tools make it possible for creative businesses to sell products and services through a polished website.</p><p><img alt="Squarespace Review 2026: Features and Pricing | Trendtrack Blog" class="sFlh5c Jm0Uqe FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="411" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6960c69bb20a3942112bc643/6a942b4d1bf5cb2b0f0bed84_6a942858daa644ccc62d7122_Squarespace-Logo.png" style="--xgflle: 1.5555555555555556; max-width: 1400px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

<p>It is particularly worth considering for businesses such as photographers, artists, designers, boutiques and independent brands that want their online store to complement a strong visual identity.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Key Squarespace ecommerce features</h3>

<ul>
<li><b>Professional templates:</b> Build a store using visually focused website designs.</li>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">How Much Does an Ecommerce Platform Cost?</h2>

<p>The monthly subscription is only one part of the cost of running an online store.</p>

<p>Depending on your platform and business model, you may also pay for hosting, payment processing, transaction fees, premium themes, apps, extensions, domain registration and fulfillment integrations.</p>

<p>Here are the main expenses to consider:</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">1. Platform subscription or hosting</h3>

<p>Hosted platforms such as Shopify, BigCommerce, Wix and Squarespace combine the core software with hosting in their subscription plans.</p>

<p>WooCommerce is different. Its core plugin is free, but you must arrange hosting separately. You may also need paid extensions or professional support.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">2. Payment processing fees</h3>

<p>Payment processors charge businesses for handling card transactions and other payment methods. Fees may include a percentage of the sale plus a fixed amount.</p>

<p>For example, a payment processor charging 2.9% plus 30¢ would collect $3.20 on a $100 transaction.</p>

<p>That is a payment processing example, not a universal rate. Your actual fees depend on the provider, payment method, card type, location and merchant agreement.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">3. Additional platform transaction fees</h3>

<p>Some platforms charge a separate transaction fee on top of payment processing when a business uses certain plans or payment providers.</p>

<p>For example:</p>

<ul>
<li>Shopify may charge an additional transaction fee when a store uses a third-party payment provider instead of Shopify Payments. The rate depends on the plan.</li>
<li>Squarespace Basic currently has a 2% online store transaction fee, while Core, Plus and Advanced have no standard online store transaction fee.</li>
<li>WooCommerce does not charge a core platform revenue share, although the selected payment processor still charges its own fees.</li>
<li>BigCommerce advertises zero platform transaction fees with supported embedded payment providers.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">4. Apps, plugins and extensions</h3>

<p>You may need additional software for email marketing, shipping, subscriptions, customer support, accounting, product reviews or advanced inventory management.</p>

<p>These costs can add up. Before installing an extension, check whether the platform already includes the feature you need.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">5. Domain, design and professional services</h3>

<p>Your business may also pay for a domain name, premium theme, logo design, product photography, store setup or developer assistance.</p>

<p>These are not always monthly expenses, but they should be included in your launch budget.</p>

<div style="background: rgb(247, 244, 252); border-radius: 10px; border: 1px solid rgb(227, 217, 242); margin: 20px 0px; padding: 18px;">
<p style="color: #45247a; font-weight: bold; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">A simple way to calculate your store's total cost</p>
<p style="margin: 0px;">Monthly platform or hosting cost + payment processing fees + additional transaction fees + app and extension subscriptions + other operating costs.</p>
<p style="color: #666666; font-size: 13px; margin: 10px 0px 0px;">Do not compare platforms using subscription prices alone. Estimate your expected order volume and average order value to understand how transaction fees affect your total cost.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">How to Choose the Right Ecommerce Platform for Your Business</h2>

<p>The best platform depends on what you sell, how you operate and how much control you want over your store.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">1. Identify what you plan to sell</h3>

<p>Are you selling physical products, digital downloads, subscriptions or services?</p>

<p>A store selling a small collection of handmade products may not need the same tools as a business managing thousands of products across multiple warehouses.</p>

<p>Make sure the platform supports your product types and the selling features you need.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">2. Consider your technical experience</h3>

<p>If you have little technical experience and want the simplest route to launching a store, hosted platforms such as Shopify, Wix and Squarespace may be easier to manage.</p>

<p>If you already use WordPress or have access to technical support, WooCommerce offers more flexibility.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">3. Calculate the full cost of ownership</h3>

<p>Compare subscriptions, payment processing, additional transaction fees, hosting, apps and extensions.</p>

<p>A platform with a lower advertised price may become more expensive when you add the features your store needs.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">4. Check payment provider availability</h3>

<p>Not every payment provider is available in every country. Before committing, confirm that the platform supports the payment methods your customers use and that you can actually receive payouts in your business location.</p>

<p>This is especially important for businesses selling internationally.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">5. Review shipping and fulfillment tools</h3>

<p>If you sell physical products, investigate shipping integrations, order tracking, fulfillment workflows, inventory synchronization and delivery options.</p>

<p>These features can become increasingly important as order volume grows.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">6. Consider search visibility and marketing</h3>

<p>Your online store needs to be discoverable. Look for useful SEO controls, mobile-friendly storefronts, marketing integrations, analytics and options for connecting social channels.</p>

<p>Do not choose a platform based on marketing claims alone. Check whether it gives you practical control over page titles, descriptions, URLs, product information and other important elements.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">7. Think about future growth</h3>

<p>Consider whether the platform can support more products, additional staff, new sales channels, international customers and more complex operations.</p>

<p>Switching platforms later can involve migration work, additional expenses and potential disruption to your store. It is worth considering your likely needs before launching.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">Ecommerce Platform vs. Website Builder: What's the Difference?</h2>

<p>The terms are often used interchangeably, but they do not always describe the same thing.</p>

<p>A <b>website builder</b> is primarily designed to help users create and manage websites. Some website builders include ecommerce tools, allowing businesses to sell products and services directly from their websites.</p>

<p>An <b>ecommerce platform</b> is designed around online selling. It typically provides tools for product catalogs, checkout, payments, inventory, orders and other commerce operations.</p>

<p>Shopify and BigCommerce are examples of dedicated ecommerce platforms. Wix and Squarespace combine website building with ecommerce features. WooCommerce adds ecommerce functionality to a WordPress website.</p>

<p>There is no universal winner. If selling products is the central purpose of your website, a dedicated ecommerce platform may offer a more suitable foundation. If you mainly need a website that also sells a smaller selection of products, a website builder with ecommerce tools may be sufficient.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">What is the best ecommerce platform for a small business?</h3>
<p>Shopify is a strong all-round option for small businesses that want a dedicated online store with built-in selling tools and room to grow. WooCommerce is a good choice for WordPress users who want flexibility, while Wix and Squarespace are suitable for businesses that prioritize website design.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">What is the cheapest ecommerce platform?</h3>
<p>WooCommerce's core plugin is free, but you still need hosting and may need paid extensions. BigCommerce, Shopify, Wix and Squarespace have paid plans. The cheapest option for your business depends on the features you need and the total cost of operating the store.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Can I start an online store for free?</h3>
<p>Some services offer free plans or free core software. However, free plans may limit product listings, payment acceptance, branding, features or integrations. WooCommerce has a free core plugin, but you must pay for hosting. Wix also offers a free website plan, but an eligible paid plan is needed to accept payments through its store.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Is Shopify better than WooCommerce?</h3>
<p>It depends on your priorities. Shopify is generally easier to manage because hosting and much of the store infrastructure are included. WooCommerce offers greater control and customization but requires you to manage hosting, updates, security and extensions.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Is BigCommerce better than Shopify?</h3>
<p>BigCommerce may suit businesses that want more built-in commerce functionality and supported payment arrangements without additional platform transaction fees. Shopify offers a larger app ecosystem and a widely used hosted commerce environment. Compare the features, sales thresholds, payment arrangements and total costs before deciding.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Can I sell digital products using these platforms?</h3>
<p>Yes, all five platforms can support certain digital-product sales, either through built-in features or extensions. However, the process, file delivery tools, subscription options and transaction fees differ. Confirm that the platform supports the type of digital product you intend to sell.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Do I need coding skills to build an ecommerce website?</h3>
<p>No. Shopify, BigCommerce, Wix and Squarespace provide tools that allow users to build stores without writing code. WooCommerce can also be used without coding, but more advanced customization and maintenance may require technical knowledge.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Can I use my own domain name?</h3>
<p>Yes. All five platforms support custom domains. Some hosted plans include a domain registration promotion for the first year, while WooCommerce users typically arrange domain registration separately through their chosen provider.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Can I change ecommerce platforms later?</h3>
<p>Yes, but migrating a store can require transferring product information, customer records, order history, URLs, content and other data. Some information may need manual adjustments, and certain integrations may need to be configured again. Plan carefully before switching.</p>

<h3 style="color: #65449a;">Which ecommerce platform is best for selling internationally?</h3>
<p>Shopify and BigCommerce offer tools for businesses selling across markets, while WooCommerce provides flexibility through extensions and payment integrations. Wix and Squarespace also support international selling features, subject to country and plan restrictions. Check supported currencies, payment methods, tax tools, shipping options and local availability before choosing.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(228, 216, 245); color: #45247a; padding-bottom: 9px;">Final Verdict: Which Ecommerce Platform Should You Choose?</h2>

<p>Each platform on this list can help a small business sell online, but they are not interchangeable.</p>

<ul>
<li><b>Choose Shopify</b> if you want a dedicated ecommerce platform with a straightforward setup process, an extensive app ecosystem and room to expand.</li>
<li><b>Choose WooCommerce</b> if you already use WordPress or want greater control over your website, hosting and store functionality.</li>
<li><b>Choose BigCommerce</b> if you want a hosted platform with substantial built-in commerce features and are planning for a more sophisticated store.</li>
<li><b>Choose Wix</b> if you want a visually customizable business website with an integrated online store.</li>
<li><b>Choose Squarespace</b> if design and brand presentation are major priorities and your store has relatively straightforward selling requirements.</li>
</ul>

<p>Before making your decision, calculate the full cost of running your store, confirm payment availability in your country and consider what your business may need as it grows.</p>

<p>The right ecommerce platform should make selling easier, not create unnecessary complexity. Choose one that supports your current business model while leaving enough room for your next stage of growth.</p>
