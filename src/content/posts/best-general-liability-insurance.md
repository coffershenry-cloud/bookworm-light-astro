<h2 style="color: #182b49; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 35px;">What Is General Liability Insurance?</h2>

<p>General liability insurance, also called commercial general liability (CGL) insurance, is a business policy designed to help protect a company against certain claims made by third parties.</p>

<p>These claims commonly involve bodily injury, property damage, and personal or advertising injury connected to the business's operations, premises or advertising activities.</p>

<p>For example, imagine a customer visits a retail store, slips on a wet floor and suffers an injury. The customer may seek compensation for medical expenses and other damages. If the business is found legally responsible, a general liability policy may help cover eligible costs, subject to the policy terms and limits.</p>

<p>Another example involves a contractor who accidentally damages a customer's property while carrying out a repair. General liability insurance may help cover the resulting claim if the incident falls within the policy's coverage.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">What does general liability insurance typically cover?</h3>

<ul>
<li><b>Bodily injury:</b> Certain injuries suffered by customers, visitors or other third parties because of covered business-related incidents.</li>
<li><b>Property damage:</b> Certain accidental damage to property belonging to someone else.</li>
<li><b>Personal and advertising injury:</b> Specified non-physical claims, such as certain allegations of libel, slander or advertising-related harm.</li>
<li><b>Legal defense costs:</b> Eligible legal expenses associated with covered claims, subject to the policy's terms.</li>
<li><b>Medical payments:</b> Certain medical expenses for injured third parties, depending on the policy.</li>
</ul>

<p>Coverage is not automatic for every accident or lawsuit. The policy wording, exclusions, limits and endorsements determine what the insurer will pay.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">What does general liability insurance usually not cover?</h3>

<p>General liability insurance is not a replacement for every type of business insurance. Some common exposures require separate policies or additional coverage.</p>

<ul>
<li><b>Professional mistakes:</b> Claims involving negligent professional advice or services generally require professional liability or errors and omissions insurance.</li>
<li><b>Employee workplace injuries:</b> These are generally addressed through workers' compensation insurance, subject to state requirements and applicable exceptions.</li>
<li><b>Business property damage:</b> Damage to the business's own building, equipment or inventory generally requires commercial property coverage.</li>
<li><b>Business vehicle accidents:</b> Commercial auto insurance is generally needed for covered business vehicle exposures.</li>
<li><b>Cyber incidents:</b> Data breaches, ransomware and other cyber-related losses generally require cyber insurance.</li>
</ul>

<p>Some insurers offer packages or endorsements that combine or extend coverage. However, a general liability policy alone should not be assumed to cover these separate risks.</p>

<h2 style="color: #182b49; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 40px;">How to Choose the Right General Liability Insurance Company</h2>

<p>The best provider for your business depends on your industry, operations, location, contract requirements and budget. Before selecting a policy, consider the following questions.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">1. What type of business do you operate?</h3>

<p>A retail store, cleaning company, construction contractor and online consultant face different liability exposures.</p>

<p>For example, a business that regularly welcomes customers onto its premises may have different injury risks from a professional who works remotely. A contractor working inside customers' homes may need to pay particular attention to third-party property damage coverage.</p>

<p>Choose a provider that can insure your actual business activities, not simply one that advertises a low starting premium.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">2. What coverage limits do you need?</h3>

<p>A policy limit is the maximum amount an insurer will pay for a covered claim or group of claims, depending on the limit structure.</p>

<p>Common general liability policies may include:</p>

<ul>
<li><b>Per-occurrence limit:</b> The maximum amount the policy will pay for one covered occurrence.</li>
<li><b>General aggregate limit:</b> The maximum amount the policy will pay for covered claims during the policy period, subject to the policy wording.</li>
</ul>

<p>Some clients and commercial landlords require businesses to carry specified limits before signing a contract or granting access to a worksite.</p>

<p>Do not choose a limit simply because it is the cheapest option. Compare the policy requirements in your contracts and consider the potential financial consequences of a serious claim.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">3. Does the policy meet your contract requirements?</h3>

<p>A client may request a certificate of insurance showing that your business carries general liability coverage.</p>

<p>They may also require a particular limit or ask to be listed as an additional insured. If this applies to your business, confirm that the insurer can provide the required documentation and endorsement.</p>

<p>A certificate of insurance is evidence of coverage, but it does not replace the actual insurance policy or change its terms.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">4. Do you need annual or short-term coverage?</h3>

<p>Businesses that operate throughout the year may prefer an annual policy. A business that works on occasional projects or seasonal assignments may want to compare flexible coverage periods.</p>

<p>However, short-term coverage should not be selected without checking the project dates and the policy's effective and expiration times. A gap between coverage periods could leave an activity uninsured.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">5. What exclusions and endorsements apply?</h3>

<p>Two policies can have the same headline limits but protect against different risks.</p>

<p>Review exclusions, endorsements, definitions and any special conditions. Pay particular attention to activities that are important to your business, such as subcontractor work, completed operations, products, contractual liability and work performed away from your premises.</p>

<p>Ask the insurer or licensed agent to explain anything you do not understand before purchasing.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 19px;">6. How does the insurer handle claims and support?</h3>

<p>Insurance is most important when something goes wrong. Before buying a policy, find out how to report a claim, how support is provided and whether you can speak with a licensed professional when needed.</p>

<p>Digital convenience can be valuable, but it should not be the only factor in your decision.</p>

<h2 style="color: #182b49; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 40px;">General Liability Insurance vs a Business Owners Policy</h2>

<p>This can be useful for a business that needs both liability protection and coverage for its own physical property.</p>

<h2 style="color: #182b49; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 40px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">What is the best general liability insurance for a small business?</h3>
<p>The best policy depends on the business's industry, location, size and coverage needs. ERGO NEXT, The Hartford, biBERK, Thimble and Hiscox are providers worth comparing. Obtain quotes using the same coverage requirements before making a decision.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">Is general liability insurance required by law?</h3>
<p>General liability insurance is not universally required for every US business by federal law. However, state or industry-specific rules, licensing arrangements, commercial leases and client contracts may require certain coverage. Check the requirements that apply to your business and location.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">What does general liability insurance cover?</h3>
<p>General liability insurance can cover certain third-party claims involving bodily injury, property damage and personal or advertising injury. It may also help pay eligible legal defense costs, settlements or judgments, subject to the policy terms and limits.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">Does general liability insurance cover professional mistakes?</h3>
<p>Generally, no. Claims involving negligent advice, professional services or errors and omissions usually require professional liability insurance. General liability and professional liability protect against different types of claims.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">How much general liability insurance coverage does a small business need?</h3>
<p>The appropriate limit depends on the business's risks, contracts, customer requirements and financial exposure. Many commercial contracts request at least $1 million in general liability coverage, but requirements vary. Review your contracts and discuss suitable limits with a licensed insurance professional.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">Can I get general liability insurance online?</h3>
<p>Yes. Providers such as ERGO NEXT, biBERK, Thimble and Hiscox offer online quote processes for eligible businesses. Some insurers also provide licensed agents or representatives who can help explain coverage options.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">Does general liability insurance cover employees?</h3>
<p>General liability insurance is primarily designed for third-party claims, not employee workplace injuries. Businesses generally need workers' compensation insurance for eligible employee injury exposures, subject to applicable state laws and exceptions.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">Can I add a client as an additional insured?</h3>
<p>Some general liability policies allow eligible clients, landlords or project partners to be added as additional insureds through an endorsement. The request must meet the insurer's requirements, and the added protection is governed by the endorsement's terms.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">Is a certificate of insurance the same as an insurance policy?</h3>
<p>No. A certificate of insurance is a document that summarizes evidence of coverage. It does not replace the policy, expand coverage or change exclusions and limits. The policy and its endorsements determine the actual protection provided.</p>

<h3 style="color: #263b5c; font-size: 18px;">Can a business buy general liability insurance for one day?</h3>
<p>Some providers, including Thimble, offer flexible coverage periods for eligible businesses. Availability depends on the type of work, policy terms and insurer underwriting. Confirm the effective dates and covered activities before relying on short-term coverage.</p>
