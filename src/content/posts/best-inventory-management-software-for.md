  <h1 style="color: #172554; font-size: 34px; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.25; margin: 0px 0px 20px;">Best Inventory Management Software for Small Businesses in 2027</h1>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">Keeping track of inventory sounds simple until your business starts growing. You have products arriving from suppliers, orders going out to customers, stock sitting in different locations and employees updating records throughout the day. Before long, a spreadsheet that once seemed perfectly adequate can become difficult to manage.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; text-align: center;"><img alt="Inventory management with barcode scanning Photos - Download Free  High-Quality Pictures | Magnific (formerly Freepik)" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="357" src="https://img.magnific.com/premium-photo/warehouse-worker-scanning-inventory-with-handheld-device_987366-83661.jpg?semt=ais_hybrid&amp;w=740&amp;q=80" style="--xgflle: 1.791767554479419; max-width: 740px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">That is where inventory management software comes in. The right system helps you monitor stock levels, record incoming and outgoing products, manage purchase orders and reduce the risk of selling items you no longer have available.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">However, not every small business needs an expensive warehouse management platform. A retailer selling products from one physical location has different requirements from an online store handling hundreds of orders or a wholesaler managing several warehouses.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 24px;">In this guide, we compare six inventory management solutions for small businesses in 2027, looking at their features, pricing, strengths and limitations. We also explain which type of business each platform is best suited for so you can choose a system that fits your actual operations.</p>

    <ul style="margin: 0px; padding-left: 22px;">

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin: 36px 0px 16px;">The 6 best inventory management software options for small businesses</h2>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">The platforms below serve different business needs. Some focus on straightforward stock tracking, while others combine inventory management with sales, purchasing, accounting or online commerce.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 23px; margin: 28px 0px 12px;">1. Zoho Inventory – Best overall for many small businesses</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Zoho Inventory is a cloud-based inventory and order management platform designed for businesses that purchase, store and sell physical products. It brings several important inventory activities into one system, making it useful for small retailers, distributors and online sellers.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Rather than tracking stock in one spreadsheet and customer orders in another, businesses can manage products, purchase orders, sales orders, shipments and inventory levels from a central dashboard.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">The platform also supports integrations with services such as Shopify, Amazon, eBay, Etsy, WooCommerce, QuickBooks Online and Xero. This can be particularly helpful when a business sells through multiple channels and needs its stock records to stay consistent.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Key features</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Inventory tracking and stock adjustments.</li>
    <li>Sales and purchase order management.</li>
    <li>Multi-warehouse management and stock transfers.</li>
    <li>Barcode generation and scanning features on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Batch and serial number tracking on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Shipping integrations and order fulfillment tools.</li>
    <li>Connections to selected ecommerce and accounting platforms.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pricing</h4>
  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Zoho Inventory offers a free plan with a limit of 50 orders, one user and one location. Its Standard plan is listed at <b>$29 per organization per month when billed annually</b>, covering up to 500 orders per month, three users and two locations. Higher plans are available for businesses with greater order volumes and more advanced requirements.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pros</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 14px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>A useful combination of inventory, purchasing and order management features.</li>
    <li>A free plan for businesses with modest order volumes.</li>
    <li>Paid pricing is based on the organization rather than charging separately for every user within the included allowance.</li>
    <li>Integrations with several popular ecommerce and accounting platforms.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Cons</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>The free plan has strict order, user and location limits.</li>
    <li>Some advanced features require higher-tier plans or paid add-ons.</li>
    <li>Businesses with complicated warehouse operations may eventually need a more specialized system.</li>
  </ul>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;"><b>Best for:</b> Small retailers, ecommerce sellers and distributors that need inventory and order management without immediately investing in a complex enterprise platform.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 24px;"><a href="https://www.zoho.com/us/inventory/" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb; font-weight: 600; text-decoration: none;" target="_blank">Visit Zoho Inventory</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 23px; margin: 30px 0px 12px;">2. Square for Retail – Best for physical retail stores</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Square for Retail is designed for businesses that sell directly to customers from a physical shop, market stall or retail counter. Its main advantage is that inventory management is connected to the point-of-sale system used to process customer purchases.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">When a customer buys an item through the system, the sale can be reflected in the business's inventory records. This reduces the need to update stock figures separately after every transaction.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Square's retail tools also include product and SKU management, vendor information, purchase orders and inventory reporting. These capabilities make it a practical option for independent retailers that want sales and stock management connected.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Key features</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Point-of-sale and product catalog management.</li>
    <li>Inventory tracking connected to retail sales.</li>
    <li>Stock intake and vendor management.</li>
    <li>Purchase order management on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Sales and inventory reporting.</li>
    <li>Multi-location retail features on higher plans.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pricing</h4>
  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Square lists its Free retail plan at <b>$0 per month per location</b>. Square Plus costs <b>$49 per month per location</b>, while Premium costs $149 per month per location. The Plus plan includes inventory management and stock intake, vendor and purchase order management, and cost-of-goods-sold reporting.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Payment processing fees also apply when customers pay through Square. These fees are separate from the software subscription and should be included when calculating the total cost of using the platform.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pros</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 14px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Combines retail sales and inventory activities.</li>
    <li>A free entry-level plan is available.</li>
    <li>Useful for stores that want product and sales information in one system.</li>
    <li>Higher plans provide additional retail management capabilities.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Cons</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Advanced inventory features require a paid plan.</li>
    <li>Subscription costs increase as you add locations on paid plans.</li>
    <li>It is primarily a retail point-of-sale solution, not a dedicated warehouse or manufacturing management platform.</li>
  </ul>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;"><b>Best for:</b> Independent retailers, boutiques, gift shops and other businesses that sell directly to customers in person.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 24px;"><a href="https://squareup.com/us/en/point-of-sale/retail" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb; font-weight: 600; text-decoration: none;" target="_blank">Visit Square for Retail</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 23px; margin: 30px 0px 12px;">3. Sortly – Best for simple inventory and equipment tracking</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Not every business needs a full order management or warehouse system. Some simply need a reliable way to record what they own, where it is stored and how much remains available.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;"><img alt="Interior Design Inventory Management Software | Sortly" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="427" src="https://media.sortly.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/26170951/sortly-interior-design-hero-image-desktop%402x-950x634.jpg" style="--xgflle: 1.498422712933754; max-width: 950px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Sortly focuses on making inventory tracking visual and straightforward. Users can organize items, attach photographs, record quantities, create folders and use mobile devices to access inventory information.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">This approach can be particularly useful for contractors, construction companies, property managers, small warehouses and businesses that need to monitor tools, equipment, supplies or materials across different locations.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Key features</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Visual inventory records with item photographs.</li>
    <li>Mobile inventory access and updates.</li>
    <li>Barcode and QR code tools on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Low-stock and date-based alerts.</li>
    <li>Inventory reports and activity tracking.</li>
    <li>Item organization and location tracking.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pricing</h4>
  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Sortly has a free plan covering 100 unique items and one user license. Its Advanced plan is advertised at $24 per month with annual billing under a promotional first-year discount, normally displayed at $49 per month. The Advanced plan includes 500 unique items and two user licenses.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">The advertised discount is important: Sortly states that the 50% discount applies only to the first year of new customer subscriptions, with a different discount applying to subsequent annual renewals. Check the checkout price and renewal terms before subscribing.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pros</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 14px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Visual organization makes it easier to identify and locate items.</li>
    <li>Mobile access is useful for teams working away from a central office.</li>
    <li>A free plan is available for small inventories.</li>
    <li>Useful for equipment, tools, supplies and general stock tracking.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Cons</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Item and user limits can make the free plan restrictive as a business grows.</li>
    <li>Advanced features are distributed across paid plans.</li>
    <li>Businesses that need detailed purchasing, sales order and accounting workflows may prefer a more comprehensive platform.</li>
  </ul>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;"><b>Best for:</b> Small teams that want a simple, visual way to track tools, supplies, equipment and physical inventory.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 24px;"><a href="https://www.sortly.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb; font-weight: 600; text-decoration: none;" target="_blank">Visit Sortly</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 23px; margin: 30px 0px 12px;">4. inFlow Inventory – Best for wholesalers and B2B businesses</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Businesses that sell products to other businesses often have more complicated inventory requirements than a small shop selling directly to consumers. They may need to manage purchase orders, customer orders, several storage locations, picking and packing, and different product quantities.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">inFlow Inventory is designed for inventory-heavy operations such as wholesale distribution, field service and product businesses. It combines inventory control with sales and purchasing workflows, helping businesses manage stock from receiving products to fulfilling customer orders.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">The platform also offers integrations with ecommerce and accounting services, including Shopify, Amazon, WooCommerce, Xero and QuickBooks Online. Its available integrations and limits depend on the selected plan.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Key features</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Real-time inventory tracking across locations.</li>
    <li>Sales order and purchase order management.</li>
    <li>Picking, packing and shipping workflows.</li>
    <li>Barcode scanning and inventory control tools.</li>
    <li>Product and customer management.</li>
    <li>Integrations with ecommerce and accounting platforms.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pricing</h4>
  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">inFlow Inventory currently advertises a starting price of <b>$99 per month when billed annually</b>. Its Lite plan is designed for smaller teams and includes two team members and one inventory location. Higher plans increase the number of users and locations and provide additional controls.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">A 14-day free trial is advertised. Businesses should also check the costs of additional users, onboarding, hardware and optional services before choosing a plan.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pros</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 14px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Designed for businesses with more involved inventory and order workflows.</li>
    <li>Supports stock management across multiple locations.</li>
    <li>Combines purchasing, sales and fulfillment activities.</li>
    <li>Connects with several popular business applications.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Cons</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>More expensive than entry-level inventory tracking tools.</li>
    <li>Additional users, hardware and services can increase the total cost.</li>
    <li>May be more functionality than a very small business actually needs.</li>
  </ul>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;"><b>Best for:</b> Wholesalers, distributors and growing B2B businesses that need structured purchasing, sales and stock management.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 24px;"><a href="https://www.inflowinventory.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb; font-weight: 600; text-decoration: none;" target="_blank">Visit inFlow Inventory</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 23px; margin: 30px 0px 12px;">5. Odoo – Best for businesses wanting an integrated business system</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Inventory management does not always operate independently from the rest of a business. Stock purchases affect expenses, sales affect revenue, and warehouse activity influences customer orders and fulfillment.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px; text-align: center;"><img alt="Inventory dashboards — Odoo 19.0 documentation" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="409" src="https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/_images/warehouse-daily-operations.png" style="--xgflle: 1.5653082549634274; max-width: 1498px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Odoo takes an integrated approach by offering applications for inventory, accounting, sales, purchasing, ecommerce, customer relationship management and other business activities.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">A small business can start with the inventory application and expand into other applications as its operations become more complex. This can reduce the need to maintain several disconnected systems, although setting up a broader business platform may require more planning than adopting a simple stock tracker.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Key features</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Inventory and warehouse management.</li>
    <li>Product and stock movement tracking.</li>
    <li>Integration with purchasing and sales applications.</li>
    <li>Connections with accounting and ecommerce workflows.</li>
    <li>Additional business applications within the same platform.</li>
    <li>Options for online hosting and more customized deployments on eligible plans.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pricing</h4>
  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Odoo offers a <b>One App Free</b> plan that allows unlimited users to use one application on Odoo Online. Its Standard plan is listed at $24.90 per user per month with annual billing, with a displayed regular monthly rate of $31.10 per user. The promotional discount shown by Odoo applies for the first 12 months for eligible initial users.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">The free option can be attractive for a business that only needs the inventory application. However, installing additional applications can move the business to a paid plan. Hosting, customization and implementation requirements should also be considered when estimating the total cost.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pros</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 14px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Inventory can operate alongside other business applications.</li>
    <li>A free one-application plan is available.</li>
    <li>Can support businesses that want to expand beyond basic stock tracking.</li>
    <li>Offers a broad set of business management capabilities.</li>
  </ul>

  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Setup and configuration can be more involved than with simpler inventory tools.</li>
    <li>Adding applications can change the subscription cost.</li>
    <li>Some businesses may need implementation assistance or technical support to configure more complex workflows.</li>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;"><b>Best for:</b> Small businesses that want inventory management connected to accounting, sales, purchasing and other business operations.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">If your business already uses Shopify to sell products online, you may not need to purchase a separate inventory platform immediately. Shopify includes inventory management features within its commerce system, allowing merchants to manage products, stock levels and orders from the same environment.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px; text-align: center;"><img alt="Inventory Management: How it Works and Tools (2026) - Shopify" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="326" src="https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0070/7032/files/shopifyadmin2.png?v=1755242028" style="--xgflle: 1.9656019656019657; max-width: 1600px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">The platform supports inventory tracking across multiple locations and sales channels. Merchants can receive stock, transfer products between locations and make inventory adjustments. Some more advanced point-of-sale capabilities require Shopify POS Pro or other eligible features.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">This makes Shopify convenient for businesses that want their online store and inventory records connected. It is not necessarily the best choice for a company that needs a standalone warehouse management system or complex manufacturing controls.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Key features</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 18px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Inventory tracking within the ecommerce platform.</li>
    <li>Stock management across multiple locations.</li>
    <li>Product variants with separate SKUs and inventory records.</li>
    <li>Inventory transfers and stock adjustments.</li>
    <li>Sales channel and order management.</li>
    <li>Integration with additional inventory applications through the Shopify ecosystem.</li>
  </ul>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pricing</h4>
  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Shopify's Basic plan is listed at <b>$29 per month when billed annually</b> in the United States. The Grow plan is $79 per month and Advanced is $299 per month with annual billing. Monthly billing costs more.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">The core Shopify plans include inventory management, but businesses should distinguish these features from the more advanced in-store capabilities offered through POS Pro, which is an additional subscription for eligible locations.</p>

  <h4 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 20px 0px 8px;">Pros</h4>
  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 14px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Inventory and ecommerce operations are connected.</li>
    <li>Supports stock management across multiple locations.</li>

    <li>Requires a Shopify subscription to use the platform.</li>
    <li>Some advanced retail inventory capabilities require additional services.</li>
    <li>Businesses with complex warehouse, wholesale or manufacturing needs may require dedicated applications.</li>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin: 38px 0px 16px;">How to choose the right inventory management software</h2>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">Choosing inventory software should begin with understanding how your business handles products. A system that works well for a small online store may be unsuitable for a wholesaler with multiple warehouses.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px; margin: 24px 0px 10px;">1. Identify what you actually need to track</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Start by identifying the products, materials or equipment you need to monitor. A retailer may need SKU and quantity tracking, while a contractor may care more about knowing which employee or job site has a particular tool.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">If your business handles products with expiration dates, serial numbers or batches, look for software that supports those specific tracking requirements.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px; margin: 24px 0px 10px;">2. Check the number of locations and users</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">A business operating from one store has different requirements from one that stores products in several warehouses. Consider how many locations need to share inventory information and how many employees require access.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">Pay attention to subscription limits. A low starting price may become less attractive when additional users, warehouses or orders require paid upgrades.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px; margin: 24px 0px 10px;">3. Look at integrations with your existing tools</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Inventory software becomes more useful when it connects to the systems your business already uses. These may include an ecommerce store, point-of-sale system, accounting software, shipping service or marketplace.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">For example, a business selling through Shopify and Amazon should check whether its inventory system can synchronize orders and stock across both channels. Otherwise, employees may still need to update information manually.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px; margin: 24px 0px 10px;">4. Understand the full subscription cost</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 16px;">Do not compare inventory software using only the advertised monthly price. Check whether the subscription is charged per user, per location, per organization or according to order volume.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">Also account for barcode scanners, label printers, integrations, onboarding, data migration and any additional applications your business may need.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px; margin: 24px 0px 10px;">5. Test the system before committing</h3>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">Use a free plan, trial or product demonstration to test the actual workflows your employees will perform. Add sample products, record a stock receipt, process a sale and check how the system updates inventory.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 24px;">A platform may look impressive in a feature comparison but still be difficult for your team to use every day. Ease of use matters because inaccurate or incomplete stock records can undermine the value of even the most advanced software.</p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin: 38px 0px 16px;">Do small businesses really need inventory management software?</h2>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">Not every small business needs a dedicated inventory platform from day one. A business with a small number of products, one storage location and very few transactions may be able to manage stock using a spreadsheet or a basic point-of-sale system.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 18px;">However, manual tracking becomes harder as the number of products, orders, employees and storage locations increases. This is when dedicated software can provide meaningful operational benefits.</p>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Inventory management software can help businesses:</p>

  <ul style="margin: 0px 0px 22px; padding-left: 22px;">
    <li>Reduce errors caused by manually updating stock records.</li>
    <li>Identify products that are running low.</li>
    <li>Keep track of incoming purchases and outgoing orders.</li>
    <li>Understand which products are selling and which are sitting in storage.</li>
    <li>Monitor stock across different locations.</li>
    <li>Make more informed purchasing and restocking decisions.</li>
  </ul>

  <p style="margin: 0px 0px 24px;">The important point is not to buy the most expensive platform. It is to choose a system that solves a real operational problem without introducing unnecessary complexity or costs.</p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin: 38px 0px 16px;">Frequently asked questions</h2>

  <div style="border-radius: 7px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 227, 239); margin: 14px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 9px;">What is the best inventory management software for a small business?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Zoho Inventory is a strong all-round option for many small businesses because it combines stock control, purchasing, sales orders and shipping features with a free plan. However, Square may be more suitable for physical retail stores, Sortly for simple equipment tracking and inFlow for wholesalers.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 7px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 227, 239); margin: 14px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 9px;">Is there free inventory management software for small businesses?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Yes. Zoho Inventory, Sortly, Square for Retail and Odoo offer free options, although their limits and capabilities differ. Some free plans restrict the number of orders, items, users or applications you can use.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 7px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 227, 239); margin: 14px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 9px;">Can I use Excel or Google Sheets instead of inventory software?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Yes. Spreadsheets can work for businesses with a small product catalog and simple stock movements. However, they usually require more manual updates and may become difficult to maintain when multiple employees, sales channels or storage locations are involved.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 7px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 227, 239); margin: 14px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 9px;">What features should inventory management software have?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Important features include stock quantity tracking, product and SKU management, stock adjustments, purchase orders, low-stock alerts, reporting and user access controls. Businesses with more complex operations may also need barcode scanning, batch tracking, serial numbers, multi-location management and ecommerce integrations.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 7px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 227, 239); margin: 14px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 9px;">What is the difference between inventory management software and warehouse management software?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Inventory management software focuses on monitoring stock quantities, purchases, sales and product movements. Warehouse management software focuses more deeply on warehouse operations such as receiving, storage locations, picking, packing and fulfillment. Some platforms offer both types of functionality.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 7px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 227, 239); margin: 14px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 9px;">How much does inventory management software cost?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Costs vary considerably. Some platforms offer free plans, while entry-level paid subscriptions can start at a few dozen dollars per month. More advanced systems can cost hundreds of dollars monthly. The final price depends on factors such as users, order volume, inventory locations, integrations and advanced features.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 7px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 227, 239); margin: 14px 0px 28px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 18px; margin: 0px 0px 9px;">Can inventory software integrate with accounting software?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Many inventory platforms integrate with accounting systems such as QuickBooks Online, Xero or their own accounting applications. These connections can help businesses coordinate purchasing, sales and financial records. Always check which integration is supported and whether it requires an additional subscription.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="background: repeat rgb(23, 37, 84); border-radius: 8px; color: white; margin: 34px 0px 28px; padding: 27px 25px;">
    <h2 style="color: white; font-size: 24px; margin: 0px 0px 13px;">Final thoughts: Choose software that fits your business</h2>
    <p style="color: #e2e8f0; margin: 0px 0px 14px;">The best inventory management software is not necessarily the one with the longest feature list. It is the one that makes it easier to keep accurate stock records, manage orders and understand what your business has available.</p>
    <p style="color: #e2e8f0; margin: 0px 0px 14px;">For many small businesses, Zoho Inventory is a sensible place to start. Retail stores may prefer Square, teams that mainly track tools and supplies may find Sortly easier to use, and wholesalers may benefit from inFlow's more structured inventory workflows.</p>
    <p style="color: #e2e8f0; margin: 0px;">Before making a decision, compare the plan limits, test the workflows and calculate the full cost of using the platform. A system that fits your current operations and can grow with your business is usually a better investment than paying for features you do not need.</p>
