  <p style="color: #475569; font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.7;">Managing a small business often means coordinating several projects, employees, deadlines and customer requests at the same time. When work is scattered across emails, spreadsheets and messaging apps, it becomes harder to see what needs attention and who is responsible for completing it.</p>

  <p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="Magnifi — Project management Teams actually use." class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="400" src="https://magnifi.space/ui-screenshots/team-dashboard.png" style="--xgflle: 1.6; max-width: 2880px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p><p>Project management software brings that work into one organised workspace. It can help business owners assign tasks, set deadlines, monitor progress, share files and keep team members informed without relying on constant follow-up messages.</p>

  <p>However, not every platform is suitable for a small business. Some offer more features than a small team needs, while others restrict important tools to expensive subscriptions. The right choice depends on your team size, workflow, budget and the type of projects you manage.</p>

  <p>In this guide, we compare six project management platforms for small businesses in 2027: ClickUp, Asana, Trello, monday.com, Zoho Projects and Basecamp. We examine their features, free plans, pricing structures, strengths and limitations to help you choose a practical solution.</p>

    <p style="margin: 0px;">Businesses that prefer customisable visual workflows may prefer <b>monday.com</b>. <b>Zoho Projects</b> offers a low-cost paid entry plan, while <b>Basecamp</b> is attractive to teams that prefer predictable account-based pricing rather than paying separately for every user.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px; padding-bottom: 10px;">1. ClickUp: Best All-in-One Project Management Software</h2>

  <p><b>Best for:</b> Small businesses that want task management, documents, collaboration and project planning in one platform.</p>

  <p><b>Starting paid price:</b> $7 per user/month on the Unlimited plan with annual billing.</p>

  <p><b>Free plan:</b> Yes, with unlimited free plan members.</p>

  <p>ClickUp is designed to bring different parts of workplace productivity into a single platform. Rather than using separate applications for task lists, project discussions, documentation and progress tracking, a business can organise much of its work within one ClickUp workspace.</p>

  <p>Teams can create tasks, assign responsibilities, establish deadlines and organise work into spaces, folders and lists. Depending on the plan, they can also use different project views, dashboards, time tracking and automation features.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Key Features</h3>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Task creation, assignment and deadline management.</li>
    <li>Multiple ways to view work, including lists, boards and calendars.</li>
    <li>Collaborative documents and project discussions.</li>
    <li>Time tracking and workload management on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Integrations with other business applications.</li>
    <li>Automation features for reducing repetitive administrative work.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">ClickUp Pricing</h3>

  <p>ClickUp offers a Free Forever plan, followed by paid plans. Its published annual-billing prices include:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Free Forever:</b> $0, with unlimited free plan members, unlimited tasks and 60MB of storage.</li>
    <li><b>Unlimited:</b> $7 per user/month, billed annually.</li>
    <li><b>Business:</b> $12 per user/month, billed annually.</li>
    <li><b>Enterprise:</b> Custom pricing.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>The free plan is useful for testing the platform, but its storage allowance is restrictive for businesses that regularly upload documents and other files. The Unlimited plan adds features such as unlimited storage, Gantt charts, native time tracking and more integrations.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Advantages and Disadvantages</h3>

  <p><b>Advantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>A broad collection of project management and collaboration tools.</li>
    <li>Free access for teams with no free-plan member limit.</li>
    <li>Several ways to organise and visualise projects.</li>
    <li>Paid plans offer features that can reduce the need for separate tools.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Disadvantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>The number of features can make the platform overwhelming for beginners.</li>
    <li>The free plan has a limited storage allowance.</li>
    <li>Teams may need time to configure their workspace and workflows properly.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Our verdict:</b> ClickUp is a strong option for a small business that wants a flexible, all-in-one workspace and is comfortable spending some time setting it up. It may be more complicated than necessary for a team that only needs a simple shared task list.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px; padding-bottom: 10px;">2. Asana: Best for Structured Task Management</h2>

  <p><b>Best for:</b> Small businesses that need clear task ownership, deadlines and organised workflows.</p>

  <p><b>Starting paid price:</b> $10.99 per user/month on the Starter plan with annual billing.</p>

  <p><b>Free plan:</b> Yes, for up to two users.</p>

  <p>Asana focuses on helping teams organise work into manageable tasks and projects. Each task can have an assigned owner, due date, description and discussion, making it easier for team members to understand what needs to be completed and when.</p>

  <p>This structure is particularly useful for businesses that coordinate recurring activities, marketing campaigns, product launches, administrative work or client projects. Instead of relying on verbal instructions or scattered email conversations, managers can create a shared view of responsibilities and progress.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Key Features</h3>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Task and project management with clear ownership.</li>
    <li>List, board and calendar views.</li>
    <li>Project timelines and Gantt views on paid plans.</li>
    <li>Reporting dashboards for tracking project progress.</li>
    <li>Forms and custom fields on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Integrations with tools such as Slack, Google Drive and Zoom.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Asana Pricing</h3>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Personal:</b> Free for up to two users.</li>
    <li><b>Starter:</b> $10.99 per user/month, billed annually, or $13.49 billed monthly.</li>
    <li><b>Advanced:</b> $24.99 per user/month, billed annually.</li>
    <li><b>Enterprise:</b> Custom pricing.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>The free Personal plan includes unlimited tasks and projects, list, board and calendar views, and basic collaboration features. However, its two-user limit makes it more appropriate for individuals or very small partnerships than a growing team.</p>

  <p>Starter is the more relevant entry point for a business with several employees. It adds features such as timeline and Gantt views, reporting dashboards, unlimited automations, forms and custom fields.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Advantages and Disadvantages</h3>

  <p><b>Advantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Clear task ownership and deadline management.</li>
    <li>Useful views for tracking project progress.</li>
    <li>Suitable for recurring and cross-functional workflows.</li>
    <li>Integrates with commonly used business applications.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Disadvantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>The free plan is limited to two users.</li>
    <li>Several useful project planning features require a paid subscription.</li>
    <li>Costs increase as more employees need paid seats.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Our verdict:</b> Asana is a good choice for small businesses that want an organised way to assign tasks and monitor responsibilities. It is especially worth considering when several people work on different parts of the same project and managers need a clear overview of progress.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px; padding-bottom: 10px;">3. Trello: Best for Simple Visual Project Management</h2>

  <p><b>Best for:</b> Small teams that want a straightforward way to organise tasks visually.</p>

  <p><b>Starting paid price:</b> $5 per user/month on Standard with annual billing.</p>

  <p><b>Free plan:</b> Yes, for up to 10 collaborators per Workspace.</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="Get started with Kanban using Trello boards | Nave" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="303" src="https://getnave.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/kanban-board-in-trello.jpg" style="--xgflle: 2.1129883843717; max-width: 2001px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>Trello uses a board-based system that makes project progress easy to understand. A typical board contains lists representing stages of work, such as To Do, In Progress and Completed. Individual tasks are represented by cards that can be moved between lists as work progresses.</p>

  <p>This approach is simple to understand and can work well for small marketing teams, freelancers, content creators and businesses that manage a manageable number of ongoing projects.</p>

  <p>For example, a small business could create a board for a product launch, with cards for preparing marketing materials, updating its website, contacting suppliers and announcing the launch. Each card can have an assigned person and due date.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Key Features</h3>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Visual project boards with lists and task cards.</li>
    <li>Task assignments and due dates.</li>
    <li>Checklists and file attachments.</li>
    <li>Power-Ups for adding integrations and extra functionality.</li>
    <li>Automation through Butler and eligible plan features.</li>
    <li>Additional project views on higher plans.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Trello Pricing</h3>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Free:</b> $0 for up to 10 collaborators per Workspace, with a limit of 10 boards per Workspace.</li>
    <li><b>Standard:</b> $5 per user/month, billed annually, or $6 billed monthly.</li>
    <li><b>Premium:</b> $10 per user/month, billed annually, or $12.50 billed monthly.</li>
    <li><b>Enterprise:</b> Pricing depends on the number of users and billing arrangement.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>The free plan can be enough for a small business that needs basic boards, cards, assignments and deadlines. Standard is worth considering when the team needs unlimited boards, advanced checklists and additional features. Premium adds more advanced views and administrative capabilities.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Advantages and Disadvantages</h3>

  <p><b>Advantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Easy-to-understand visual interface.</li>
    <li>Free plan accommodates up to 10 collaborators.</li>
    <li>Useful for organising straightforward projects.</li>
    <li>Paid entry plan is relatively affordable.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Disadvantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Complex projects may require additional tools or Power-Ups.</li>
    <li>Basic boards may not provide enough detail for advanced resource planning.</li>
    <li>Some useful views and administrative features require higher plans.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Our verdict:</b> Trello is a sensible option for a business that wants to replace scattered task lists with a shared visual board. It is particularly suitable when simplicity matters more than advanced reporting, resource management or complicated project dependencies.</p>

  <p><b>Best for:</b> Small businesses that want visual project tracking and customisable workflows.</p>

  <p><b>Starting paid price:</b> $9 per seat/month on Basic with annual billing.</p>

  <p><b>Free plan:</b> Yes, for up to two seats.</p>

  <p>monday.com is built around customisable boards that allow teams to organise work using columns, statuses, dates, owners and other information. Rather than forcing every business to follow the same project structure, the platform allows users to adapt boards to their own processes.</p>

  <p>A marketing agency, for example, could build a board to track client campaigns, deadlines, approval stages and assigned employees. A retail business might use boards to monitor operational tasks, supplier activities and internal projects.</p>

  <p>The platform's visual layout can make it easier for managers to understand project status without opening individual task records one by one.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Key Features</h3>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Customisable boards and project workflows.</li>
    <li>Task ownership, statuses and deadlines.</li>
    <li>Calendar and timeline views on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Gantt charts and automation features on eligible plans.</li>
    <li>Integrations with other business applications.</li>
    <li>Dashboards and reporting capabilities on higher plans.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">monday.com Pricing</h3>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Free:</b> $0 for up to two seats, with up to three boards.</li>
    <li><b>Basic:</b> $9 per seat/month with annual billing.</li>
    <li><b>Standard:</b> $12 per seat/month with annual billing.</li>
    <li><b>Pro:</b> $19 per seat/month with annual billing.</li>
    <li><b>Enterprise:</b> Custom pricing.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Basic provides a starting point for businesses that need a more flexible workspace than the free plan. Standard adds features such as timeline and Gantt views, guest access and additional automation and integration allowances. Pro adds more advanced workflow and reporting capabilities.</p>

  <p>One important consideration is seat selection. monday.com's pricing page calculates the total according to the number of seats selected, and businesses should check the available seat options and checkout total rather than assuming the displayed per-seat price is the complete monthly bill.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Advantages and Disadvantages</h3>

  <p><b>Advantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Flexible boards that can be adapted to different business processes.</li>
    <li>Visual project tracking and status monitoring.</li>
    <li>Useful for teams coordinating different types of work.</li>
    <li>Higher plans offer additional automation and reporting features.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Disadvantages</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>The free plan is limited to two seats.</li>
    <li>Seat selection and plan restrictions can affect the actual cost.</li>
    <li>Some advanced capabilities require more expensive subscriptions.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Our verdict:</b> monday.com is worth considering for small businesses that prefer visual workflows and want to customise how projects are organised. Before subscribing, calculate the cost for the entire team and confirm that the plan includes the views and automation features you need.</p>

    <li>Useful project planning features, including Gantt charts.</li>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">

    <li>Some advanced features are reserved for higher plans.</li>
    <li>Businesses outside the Zoho ecosystem may not benefit from every integration.</li>

  <p>Before purchasing a subscription, consider how your business operates. A platform that works well for a five-person marketing agency may not be the right choice for a construction company, online retailer or consultancy.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">1. Identify Your Main Project Management Needs</h3>

  <p>Start by identifying the problems you want the software to solve. Do employees regularly miss deadlines? Are project responsibilities unclear? Do you struggle to track client work? Are important files and updates spread across multiple applications?</p>

  <p>Write down the tasks that need to be managed before comparing platforms. This helps prevent paying for advanced features that the business will rarely use.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">2. Consider Your Team Size</h3>

  <p>Some tools charge per user, while others offer account-based packages with a fixed number of users included. A per-user subscription may be affordable for a team of three but become more expensive as the business hires additional employees.</p>

  <p>Check the pricing for your current team size and estimate what the subscription would cost if the team doubled. This is especially important for businesses expecting to grow during 2027.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">3. Examine Free Plan Restrictions</h3>

  <p>A free plan can be useful for testing software, but it may restrict the number of users, projects, boards, storage space or available views.</p>

  <p>For example, Trello's free plan supports up to ten collaborators, while Asana's Personal plan supports two users. Zoho Projects allows up to five users on its free plan, and ClickUp's Free Forever plan has unlimited free members but a limited storage allowance.</p>

  <p>Consider whether the free version can support your actual workflow rather than choosing a platform simply because it advertises a free plan.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">4. Check Integrations With Your Existing Tools</h3>

  <p>Project management software should work with the applications your employees already use. Depending on your business, this may include email, cloud storage, communication platforms, accounting software, customer relationship management systems or design applications.</p>

  <p>Integrations can reduce duplicate data entry and make it easier for employees to access the information they need. Check whether the integration is included in your chosen plan or requires an additional subscription.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">5. Look at Reporting and Progress Tracking</h3>

  <p>Business owners need to know whether projects are progressing as expected. Depending on the complexity of your work, useful features may include dashboards, Gantt charts, workload views, time tracking and project reports.</p>

  <p>A team that manages simple weekly tasks may not need advanced reporting. However, a business coordinating multiple client projects or employees may benefit from a platform that provides a clearer overview of deadlines and workloads.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">6. Test the Software Before Committing</h3>

  <p>Take advantage of free plans or trials where available. Add a few real projects, invite relevant team members and test the workflow before moving all business activities into the platform.</p>

  <p>Pay attention to how quickly employees understand the interface. A tool with hundreds of features is not useful if the team finds it difficult to use consistently.</p>
