  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 35px;">Can You Get Travel Insurance After Age 70?</h2>

  <p>Yes. People over 70 can still purchase travel insurance, including policies that cover international trips, cruises and domestic holidays. However, the number of available plans may become smaller as travellers get older.</p>

  <p>There is no single maximum age that applies to every travel insurance company. Each insurer sets its own eligibility rules, and restrictions can differ between policies from the same provider. Some plans may accept travellers in their 80s or older, while others impose lower age limits or reduce certain benefit limits for older applicants.</p>

  <p>For this reason, do not assume that a policy advertised for seniors automatically covers someone who is 75, 80 or 85. Enter the traveller's exact date of birth when requesting a quote and read the policy certificate to confirm eligibility.</p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 38px;">What Should Seniors Over 70 Look for in Travel Insurance?</h2>

  <p>A policy with an impressive headline coverage limit is not necessarily the right choice. Before purchasing, pay attention to the following benefits and conditions.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px;">1. Emergency Medical Coverage</h3>

  <p>Emergency medical coverage can help pay for eligible treatment when an unexpected illness or injury occurs during a trip. Depending on the policy, covered expenses may include hospital treatment, doctor visits, emergency tests and other medically necessary services.</p>

  <p>For international travel, compare plans with substantial medical limits. Squaremouth recommends at least $100,000 in emergency medical coverage for older travellers taking international trips. A higher limit may be sensible when visiting destinations with expensive healthcare or travelling far from major medical facilities.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px;">2. Emergency Medical Evacuation</h3>

  <p>Medical evacuation coverage is particularly important when travelling to remote areas, taking cruises or visiting countries where suitable medical facilities may be far away.</p>

  <p>If a treating medical professional and the insurer's assistance team determine that an emergency transfer is medically necessary, the policy may cover eligible transportation to an appropriate medical facility. Some policies may also cover medically necessary transportation back home.</p>

  <p>Do not assume that every evacuation benefit pays for transportation to the hospital of your choice. Policies can specify where the patient may be transported, who makes the decision and which expenses qualify.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px;">3. Pre-Existing Medical Condition Coverage</h3>

  <p>Many standard travel insurance policies exclude losses connected to pre-existing medical conditions unless the policy provides a qualifying waiver or another form of coverage.</p>

  <p>This matters to older travellers who may have ongoing medical conditions or a recent history of treatment. A pre-existing condition waiver can remove the policy's exclusion for qualifying conditions, but only when the traveller satisfies the insurer's requirements.</p>

  <p>Common requirements include:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Purchasing the policy within a specified number of days after the first trip deposit.</li>
    <li>Being medically fit to travel when the policy is purchased.</li>
    <li>Insuring the full eligible prepaid, non-refundable trip cost.</li>
    <li>Adding later travel purchases within the insurer's specified deadline.</li>
    <li>Meeting the policy's definition of a pre-existing condition and any stability-period requirements.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Deadlines differ between insurers and plans. Missing a waiver deadline may mean that a medical condition remains excluded, even if the traveller has purchased other travel insurance benefits.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px;">4. Primary vs. Secondary Medical Coverage</h3>

  <p>Travel medical insurance may be described as primary or secondary coverage.</p>

  <p><b>Primary coverage</b> generally allows you to submit an eligible medical claim to the travel insurer without first filing it with another health insurance provider.</p>

  <p><b>Secondary coverage</b> generally requires you to seek payment from your primary health insurance first. The travel insurer may then consider eligible remaining expenses according to its policy terms.</p>

  <p>For seniors with Medicare, this distinction can be important when travelling abroad. Original Medicare usually provides very limited coverage outside the United States. A secondary travel policy may therefore involve additional claim steps even when Medicare does not pay the overseas bill.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px;">5. Trip Cancellation and Interruption</h3>

  <p>Medical protection is not the only reason to buy travel insurance. A serious illness, injury or other covered event could force you to cancel a holiday before departure or return home earlier than planned.</p>

  <p>Trip cancellation coverage may reimburse eligible prepaid, non-refundable expenses when the reason for cancellation is covered. Trip interruption coverage may reimburse certain unused trip expenses and additional transportation costs when a covered event interrupts the journey.</p>

  <p>Check the policy's covered reasons and maximum reimbursement amounts. A policy that advertises 100% trip cancellation coverage does not mean you can cancel for any reason and receive a full refund.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 20px;">6. 24-Hour Emergency Assistance</h3>

  <p>Access to an emergency assistance service can be valuable when a medical problem occurs in another country. Check whether the insurer offers round-the-clock assistance and whether its service can help locate medical facilities, coordinate eligible transportation and explain the claims process.</p>

  <p>Save the assistance telephone number and policy details before departure. Do not wait until an emergency to discover how to contact the insurer.</p>

  <p>These figures are examples, not guaranteed 2027 prices. Your quote may be higher or lower depending on your personal circumstances and the policy available in your state.</p>

  <p>To manage the cost, consider these steps:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Compare several insurers rather than accepting the first quote.</li>
    <li>Insure eligible prepaid, non-refundable trip expenses rather than costs you can recover elsewhere.</li>
    <li>Compare medical-only policies if you mainly need emergency medical and evacuation protection.</li>
    <li>Check whether an existing credit card already provides some trip cancellation or interruption benefits.</li>
    <li>Compare annual multi-trip insurance if you travel several times a year.</li>
    <li>Do not reduce essential medical or evacuation limits simply to obtain the cheapest premium.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 38px;">Does Medicare Cover Seniors Travelling Abroad?</h2>

  <p>Original Medicare generally does not cover healthcare received outside the United States, although a few narrow exceptions apply. This can leave older travellers responsible for substantial medical bills if they need treatment overseas.</p>

  <p>Some Medicare Supplement Insurance (Medigap) policies provide limited emergency medical coverage during foreign travel. Medicare Advantage plans may also offer additional overseas benefits, depending on the plan. Travellers should contact their insurer before departure to confirm exactly what is covered, including any limits, deductibles and time restrictions.</p>

  <p>Travel insurance can help fill some of these gaps, but not every travel insurance policy includes medical coverage. Read the benefit schedule carefully and confirm whether emergency treatment, evacuation and pre-existing medical conditions are covered.</p>

  <div style="background: repeat rgb(239, 246, 255); border-left-color: rgb(37, 99, 235); border-left-style: solid; border-left-width: 5px; border-left: 5px solid rgb(37, 99, 235); border-radius: 5px; margin: 25px 0px; padding: 18px 20px;">
    <p style="color: #1e3a8a; margin: 0px;"><b>Important:</b> Do not assume that Medicare, a Medicare Advantage plan or a Medigap policy will cover every medical expense abroad. Confirm your existing health coverage before travelling and compare it with the benefits offered by a separate travel insurance policy.</p>
  </div>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 25px; margin-top: 38px;">How to Choose the Right Travel Insurance Policy</h2>

  <p>Before buying, use this checklist to compare the actual policy documents rather than relying only on a provider's marketing summary.</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Confirm the age limit:</b> Make sure the insurer accepts the traveller's exact age on the relevant dates.</li>
    <li><b>Check medical limits:</b> Compare emergency medical coverage and any age-related reductions.</li>
    <li><b>Review evacuation coverage:</b> Confirm the maximum benefit and the conditions for arranging transportation.</li>
    <li><b>Check existing conditions:</b> Read the waiver deadline, medical stability rules and eligibility requirements.</li>
    <li><b>Review cancellation protection:</b> Understand which events qualify and how much of the trip cost is insured.</li>
    <li><b>Check the deductible:</b> Find out how much you may need to pay before eligible benefits apply.</li>
    <li><b>Review exclusions:</b> Look for restrictions involving medical treatment, activities, destinations and travel against medical advice.</li>
    <li><b>Save emergency contact details:</b> Keep the insurer's assistance number and policy information accessible while travelling.</li>
  </ul>
