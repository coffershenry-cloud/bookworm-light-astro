    Personal auto insurance is primarily designed for everyday driving, while commercial auto insurance is designed for vehicles used in business operations. Although the two types of policies share some coverage features, they are not interchangeable in every situation.
  </p>

  <p>
    A freelancer who occasionally drives to client meetings may have different insurance needs from a delivery company operating several vans. A contractor carrying expensive equipment may also need protection that a standard personal auto policy does not provide.

    <b style="color: #172554;">Quick answer: What is the difference?</b>
    <p style="margin: 8px 0px 0px;">

Commercial auto insurance is designed for vehicles used in business activities, such as deliveries, transporting equipment, visiting job sites and carrying goods or passengers. If you regularly use a vehicle for work, check your personal policy before assuming you are covered.

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 38px;">What Is Personal Auto Insurance?</h2>

  <p>
    Personal auto insurance is a policy designed to protect individuals against certain financial losses involving their personal vehicles.
  </p>

  <p>
    It typically covers activities such as driving to the grocery store, visiting friends, taking family trips and commuting to work. Depending on the policy, it may also provide protection against accidents, vehicle theft, weather damage and injuries.
  </p>

  <p>
    Personal auto policies commonly include several coverage options:
  </p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Liability coverage:</b> Helps pay for injuries or property damage you cause to other people in an accident for which you are legally responsible.</li>
    <li><b>Collision coverage:</b> Helps pay to repair or replace your vehicle after a covered collision, subject to the policy deductible.</li>
    <li><b>Comprehensive coverage:</b> Helps cover certain non-collision losses, such as theft, vandalism, fire or weather-related damage.</li>
    <li><b>Uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage:</b> May help protect you when an at-fault driver has no insurance or insufficient insurance.</li>
    <li><b>Medical payments or personal injury protection:</b> Depending on the state and policy, may help pay eligible medical expenses and other costs after an accident.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    The exact protection depends on the coverage selected, policy wording and applicable state requirements.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 38px;">What Is Commercial Auto Insurance?</h2>

  <p>
    Commercial auto insurance, also known as business auto insurance, is designed to protect vehicles used for business activities.
  </p>

  <p>
    Businesses may need this coverage for cars, pickup trucks, vans, trailers and other eligible vehicles used to perform work.
  </p>

  <p>
    For example, a cleaning company may use vans to transport employees and supplies between customer locations. A construction contractor may use a pickup truck to carry tools to building sites. A restaurant may operate vehicles for food deliveries.
  </p>

  <p>
    Commercial auto insurance can provide liability and physical damage protection for covered business vehicles. Depending on the insurer and policy, additional options may include uninsured motorist coverage, medical payments, rental reimbursement and towing assistance.
  </p>

  <p>
    Commercial policies may also accommodate multiple employee drivers and vehicles, subject to the insurer's eligibility requirements and driver-listing rules.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">Commercial Auto Insurance vs Personal Auto Insurance: Key Differences</h2>

  <p>
    The biggest difference is the purpose for which the vehicle is used. Personal policies are designed around individual driving needs, while commercial policies are designed to address the additional risks associated with business operations.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">When Is Personal Auto Insurance Not Enough for Business Use?</h2>

  <p>
    One of the most important mistakes business owners make is assuming that a personal auto policy automatically covers every activity involving their vehicle.
  </p>

  <p>
    Some personal policies may allow limited business-related driving, while others restrict or exclude particular commercial activities. The distinction depends on the insurer, policy wording and type of work being performed.
  </p>

  <p>
    You should contact your insurer if your vehicle is used for activities such as:
  </p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Making regular deliveries for a business.</li>
    <li>Transporting products or inventory for customers.</li>
    <li>Carrying tools and equipment to different job sites.</li>
    <li>Transporting passengers for payment.</li>
    <li>Allowing employees to drive your vehicle for business activities.</li>
    <li>Operating as part of a courier or transportation service.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Occasional travel to a client meeting may be treated differently from using a vehicle as an essential part of a delivery business. Do not assume that all business use is automatically excluded or automatically covered.
  </p>

  <p>
    Progressive explains the differences between personal and commercial auto insurance in its <a href="https://www.progressivecommercial.com/commercial-auto-insurance/commercial-vs-personal/" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb;" target="_blank">commercial versus personal auto insurance guide</a>.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">Does Personal Auto Insurance Cover Business Deliveries?</h2>

  <p>
    Not necessarily. Delivering products, food or other goods for a business can create additional insurance risks that may not be covered by a standard personal auto policy.
  </p>

  <p>
    For example, imagine a restaurant employee who uses a personal car to deliver meals to customers. If the employee causes an accident during a delivery, the personal insurer may investigate whether the activity is covered under the policy.
  </p>

  <p>
    The same issue can arise when a small business owner uses a personal pickup truck to transport customer orders or supplies.
  </p>

  <p>
    Businesses that rely on employee-owned vehicles may need to consider hired and non-owned auto coverage. This can help protect the business against certain liability claims arising from employees using personal or rented vehicles for work.
  </p>

  <p>
    Importantly, hired and non-owned auto coverage generally protects the business's liability exposure. It does not automatically provide collision or comprehensive protection for an employee's personal vehicle.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">Insurance for Company-Owned Vehicles</h2>

  <p>
    If a business owns or leases vehicles for its operations, commercial auto insurance is commonly the appropriate starting point.
  </p>

  <p>
    The policy can be arranged to cover eligible company vehicles and authorised drivers. The business should provide accurate information about the vehicles, how they are used and who will operate them.
  </p>

  <p>Consider these examples:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Delivery company:</b> A business owns five vans used to deliver packages. Its policy should reflect the delivery activity, vehicles, drivers and operating area.</li>
    <li><b>Construction business:</b> A contractor owns pickup trucks used to transport workers, tools and building materials to job sites.</li>
    <li><b>Cleaning company:</b> A business operates vehicles that employees use to travel between customer properties with cleaning supplies.</li>
    <li><b>Retail business:</b> A shop owns a van used to move inventory between warehouses and store locations.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    A commercial auto policy generally protects the covered vehicle and certain liability exposures. It does not automatically insure everything transported inside the vehicle. Tools, inventory and other business property may require separate coverage.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">What If Employees Drive Their Own Vehicles for Business?</h2>

  <p>
    Not every business owns a fleet of vehicles. Some employers ask workers to use their personal cars for occasional business errands, customer visits or deliveries.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="" height="443" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiqiimyvaSy4Rx4Jg0cOwpWpaarIiqUgrM9oyHxb4nWV5jo-ZzJ0Wc_Di6HuJh0EXN7nr7U61llZ4siTLo_HgNuv2NXWvIb4pZxW1fzgFnvlCX4aI56X1ObDrclkpuMmcUbYioxE00WPuurxHLwm5u5Ro3Md7lV8Wu4cDlwdycpVsS4eXNg73fspp6tUr4/w640-h443/image.png" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>
    This arrangement creates a potential exposure for the employer. If an employee causes an accident while performing work duties, the business could face a liability claim depending on the circumstances and applicable law.
  </p>

  <p>
    Hired and non-owned auto insurance, commonly abbreviated as HNOA, is designed to address certain business liability exposures involving vehicles the company does not own.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">What Is Hired Auto Coverage?</h3>

  <p>
    Hired auto coverage generally relates to vehicles a business rents, hires or leases for work purposes. For example, a company may rent a car for an employee travelling to another city for a business meeting.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">What Is Non-Owned Auto Coverage?</h3>

  <p>
    Non-owned auto coverage generally relates to vehicles the business does not own but that employees use for business activities. An example would be an employee using a personal car to visit a customer or deliver a document.
  </p>

  <p>
    These coverages are often discussed together as HNOA insurance. They generally focus on liability protection for the business rather than physical damage to the rented or employee-owned vehicle.
  </p>

  <p>
    You can learn more from <a href="https://www.travelers.com/business-insurance/commercial-auto/hired-non-owned-auto-coverages" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb;" target="_blank">Travelers' explanation of hired and non-owned auto coverage</a>.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">What Coverage Does Commercial Auto Insurance Include?</h2>

  <p>
    Commercial auto insurance can include several types of protection. The options available depend on the insurer, vehicle and business activity.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">1. Commercial Auto Liability Coverage</h3>

  <p>
    Liability coverage helps protect a business when an insured driver is legally responsible for an accident that injures another person or damages someone else's property.
  </p>

  <p>
    It generally includes:
  </p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Bodily injury liability:</b> Helps pay eligible costs associated with injuries or death caused to other people.</li>
    <li><b>Property damage liability:</b> Helps pay for damage caused to another person's vehicle or property.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Liability coverage may also help pay legal defence costs, depending on the policy.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">2. Collision Coverage</h3>

  <p>
    Collision coverage can help pay to repair or replace a covered business vehicle after a collision with another vehicle or object, subject to the policy terms and deductible.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">3. Comprehensive Coverage</h3>

  <p>
    Comprehensive coverage can help pay for certain non-collision losses involving a covered vehicle. Examples may include theft, vandalism, fire, falling objects and certain weather-related damage.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">4. Uninsured and Underinsured Motorist Coverage</h3>

  <p>
    This coverage may help protect eligible insured people when an accident involves another driver who has no insurance or insufficient insurance. Availability and requirements vary by state.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">5. Medical Payments or Personal Injury Protection</h3>

  <p>
    Depending on the state and policy, medical payments or personal injury protection may help cover certain medical expenses and other eligible costs following an accident.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">6. Rental Reimbursement and Towing</h3>

  <p>
    Some policies offer additional options that help pay for a temporary rental vehicle or towing after a covered incident. These options can be especially useful for businesses that depend on their vehicles to operate.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">What Factors Affect Commercial Auto Insurance Premiums?</h2>

  <p>
    Insurers evaluate several details about a business before determining its commercial auto insurance premium.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">1. Type of Business</h3>

  <p>
    A business that operates delivery vehicles or transports goods may have a different risk profile from a consultancy whose employees occasionally visit clients.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">2. Vehicle Type and Value</h3>

  <p>
    A standard sedan, pickup truck, cargo van and heavy commercial truck do not carry the same risks or repair costs. Vehicle size, value, age and intended use can all influence premiums.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">3. Driving History</h3>

  <p>
    Insurers may review the driving records of employees who operate business vehicles. Previous accidents, traffic violations and other driving incidents can influence the cost of coverage.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">4. Mileage and Driving Frequency</h3>

  <p>
    Businesses that spend more time on the road may have greater exposure to accidents. The distance travelled, number of trips and operating areas can therefore affect premiums.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">5. Location</h3>

  <p>
    Where the business operates matters. Traffic density, accident frequency, theft exposure, repair costs and state insurance requirements can influence pricing.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">6. Number of Vehicles and Drivers</h3>

  <p>
    A company insuring several vehicles and multiple drivers may have different insurance needs from a sole proprietor operating one pickup truck. More vehicles can increase the overall cost of the policy.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">7. Coverage Limits and Deductibles</h3>

  <p>
    Higher liability limits generally increase the premium. Selecting a higher deductible for eligible physical damage coverage may reduce the upfront cost, but it also means the business could pay more out of pocket after a covered loss.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">How Do Driving Activities Affect Insurance Requirements?</h2>

  <p>
    The way a vehicle is used can be just as important as who owns it. Insurers need to understand whether a vehicle is used for occasional business errands, regular deliveries, transporting equipment or carrying passengers</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="" height="417" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEivCrb0feltZK2uc-Yd0yEV1GqXlDseu05bAnoWJz0ivyVJHsX9EPNNugcsfmzSV1Z_rlRnS3HAQhdvDUd2Y-WrQIZPt0vr1eX6CAUCtSWjuuzt4_MM5SWwo4L48-ojVH-Isk6op_08GN0jRo3EXff6IknR1aXzRMzEZ6HVZKGctC_f4zmr9RPgSbnbUqs/w640-h417/image.png" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>Consider these examples:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Consultant visiting clients:</b> Occasional business travel may be treated differently from operating a delivery service. The driver should confirm that the personal policy permits the activity.</li>
    <li><b>Food delivery business:</b> Regularly transporting meals for customers may require commercial auto coverage or a specialised delivery arrangement.</li>
    <li><b>Contractor carrying tools:</b> A pickup truck used to travel between construction sites may need commercial coverage. The tools themselves may require separate insurance.</li>
    <li><b>Passenger transportation:</b> Carrying passengers for payment may require specialised commercial or livery insurance rather than an ordinary business auto policy.</li>
    <li><b>Employee using a rented vehicle:</b> The business may need hired auto liability coverage to address certain risks involving rented vehicles.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Businesses operating commercial trucks across state lines may also face federal financial responsibility requirements. The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration explains insurance filing requirements for motor carriers and other regulated entities in its <a href="https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/insurance-filing-requirements" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb;" target="_blank">official insurance filing guide</a>.
  </p>

  <p>
    These federal requirements apply to specified operations and are not a universal rule for every business vehicle. State insurance laws and the vehicle's actual use must also be considered.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">Which Insurance Providers Offer Commercial Auto Coverage?</h2>

  <p>
    Several insurance companies offer commercial auto products or help businesses arrange this type of coverage. Availability, eligible vehicle types and policy features vary by state and business.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">How to Choose the Right Auto Insurance for Your Business</h2>

  <p>
    The right policy depends on how your vehicle is used, who drives it and what financial risks your business needs to manage.
  </p>

  <p>Use the following steps to make a more informed decision.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">Step 1: Identify How the Vehicle Is Used</h3>

  <p>
    Decide whether the vehicle is used only for personal activities, occasional business travel or regular commercial operations. Be specific about deliveries, job-site visits, transporting equipment and carrying passengers.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">Step 2: Confirm Who Owns the Vehicle</h3>

  <p>
    Determine whether the vehicle is personally owned, business-owned, leased or rented. Ownership can affect which policy structure is appropriate.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">Step 3: Identify Every Regular Driver</h3>

  <p>
    Make a list of employees and other individuals who may operate the vehicle. Ask the insurer how drivers must be listed and whether occasional drivers are eligible.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">Step 4: Review Liability Requirements</h3>

  <p>
    Check your state's minimum insurance requirements and any additional obligations created by your business contracts or industry. Businesses transporting goods or passengers may face requirements beyond those applying to ordinary private vehicles.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">Step 5: Compare Coverage and Quotes</h3>

  <p>
    Request quotes from more than one provider. Compare liability limits, collision and comprehensive coverage, deductibles, exclusions, driver restrictions and additional options.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px;">Step 6: Check What Is Not Covered</h3>

  <p>
    Ask whether the policy covers the specific activities your business performs. Also confirm whether equipment, tools, inventory and goods transported in the vehicle need separate insurance.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">Questions to Ask Before Buying Commercial Auto Insurance</h2>

  <p>
    Before purchasing a policy, consider asking the insurer or broker these questions:
  </p>

  <ol style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Does the policy cover every type of business activity we perform?</li>
    <li>Are all employees who drive our vehicles eligible for coverage?</li>
    <li>Do occasional drivers need to be listed separately?</li>
    <li>Does the policy cover deliveries, transporting equipment or travelling between job sites?</li>
    <li>Are personally owned vehicles used by employees covered through an HNOA option?</li>
    <li>Does the policy include collision and comprehensive coverage?</li>
    <li>Are tools, equipment and inventory inside the vehicle covered?</li>
    <li>What liability limits are available, and are they sufficient for our contracts?</li>
    <li>What deductibles, exclusions and driver restrictions apply?</li>
    <li>What happens if we add another vehicle or employee during the policy period?</li>
  </ol>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Is commercial auto insurance more expensive than personal auto insurance?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      It often is because commercial policies may cover higher business-related risks, multiple drivers and vehicles, and more frequent driving. However, the actual premium depends on the vehicle, business activity, location, driving history and coverage selected.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Can I use commercial auto insurance for personal driving?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      Some commercial auto policies cover eligible vehicles during both business and personal use. However, this depends on the policy wording and the insurer's conditions. Confirm that personal use is permitted before relying on the coverage.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Can I insure my personal car with a commercial auto policy?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      In many cases, a personally owned vehicle used for business can be insured under a commercial auto policy, subject to eligibility and ownership requirements. Some insurers allow this arrangement, while others have restrictions on certain vehicles or uses.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Do small businesses need commercial auto insurance?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      A small business may need commercial auto insurance if it owns vehicles used for business, makes deliveries, transports equipment or regularly uses vehicles for other commercial activities. The appropriate coverage depends on the vehicle, activity and applicable state requirements.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Does commercial auto insurance cover tools and equipment inside a vehicle?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      Generally, commercial auto insurance covers the insured vehicle rather than automatically covering all the property transported inside it. Businesses may need separate tools and equipment or inland marine coverage for those items.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Does commercial auto insurance cover employees driving company vehicles?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      It can cover eligible employees driving covered business vehicles, provided the policy permits their use and any driver-listing requirements are satisfied. Businesses should give insurers accurate information about all regular drivers.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">What if my employee uses a personal car for work?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      The employee's personal policy may provide some protection, depending on its terms and the activity. The employer may also need hired and non-owned auto coverage to address certain business liability exposures. The two forms of protection serve different purposes.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Is commercial auto insurance legally required?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      Most states require liability insurance for vehicles operated on public roads, but the exact requirements depend on the vehicle and its use. Certain commercial transportation activities also face additional state or federal insurance requirements. Check the rules that apply to your business.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Can freelancers and independent contractors get commercial auto insurance?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      Yes. Freelancers and independent contractors may be able to purchase commercial auto insurance when they use eligible vehicles for work. For example, a contractor who transports tools between job sites may need business vehicle coverage.
    </p>
  </div>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">Final Thoughts: Which Type of Auto Insurance Do You Need?</h2>

  <p>
    Choosing between commercial and personal auto insurance comes down to understanding how your vehicle is used and what risks your business faces.
  </p>

  <p>
    Personal auto insurance is generally appropriate for ordinary private driving and commuting, subject to the policy terms. Commercial auto insurance is designed for business vehicles and activities such as deliveries, transporting equipment, visiting job sites and operating company vehicles.
  </p>

  <p>
    If employees use their own vehicles for business, hired and non-owned auto coverage may also be worth considering. It can address certain liability exposures that a standard commercial auto policy covering company-owned vehicles may not address.
  </p>

  <p>
    Before choosing a policy, describe your actual driving activities to an insurer, confirm which drivers and vehicles are covered, and compare quotes using similar limits and coverage options. The cheapest policy is not necessarily the right one if it leaves important business activities uninsured.
  </p>
