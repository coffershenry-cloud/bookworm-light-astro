Professional liability focuses on claims connected to the professional services, advice, or expertise a business provides.<br /><br />A business can need one of these policies or both, depending on its industry and the work it performs.<br /><br />What Is General Liability Insurance?<br /><br />General liability insurance provides coverage for certain third party claims involving bodily injury, property damage, personal and advertising injury, and related legal expenses, subject to the policy terms.<br /><br />Imagine a customer visits your business and slips on a wet floor. The customer suffers an injury and files a claim against your company.<br /><br />General liability insurance may respond to that type of claim when the circumstances fall within the policy’s coverage.<br /><br />Another example involves property damage.<br /><br />A contractor working at a customer’s property accidentally damages part of the building. A general liability policy may provide coverage for an eligible third party property damage claim.<br /><br />General liability therefore focuses heavily on risks that can arise from your business operations and interactions with customers, visitors, and other third parties.<br /><br />What Is Professional Liability Insurance?<br /><br />Professional liability insurance addresses certain claims related to professional services.<br /><br />A client may claim that your advice, work, or professional service caused them financial harm.<br /><br />For example, a consultant provides advice that a client believes resulted in a significant financial loss. The client could make a professional negligence claim against the consultant.<br /><br />Professional liability coverage can help with eligible claims of this nature, depending on the policy.<br /><br />This type of insurance is particularly relevant to businesses whose primary product is their knowledge, advice, expertise, or professional service.<br /><br />General Liability vs Professional Liability<br /><br />The simplest distinction is the type of claim involved.<br /><br />General liability generally addresses certain third party claims involving physical injury, property damage, and personal or advertising injury.<br /><br />Professional liability generally addresses certain claims arising from professional services, errors, omissions, negligence, or mistakes.<br /><br />Consider a graphic designer.<br /><br />A client visits the designer’s office and trips over a piece of equipment. The resulting bodily injury claim could fall under general liability coverage.<br /><br />Now imagine the designer delivers work that contains an error and the client claims that the mistake caused financial losses. That situation may involve professional liability.<br /><br />The exact coverage depends on the policy wording and circumstances of the claim.<br /><br />Examples of General Liability Claims<br /><br />General liability can apply to several common business situations.<br /><br />Customer Injuries<br /><br />A customer slips and falls inside your business premises.<br /><br />Property Damage<br /><br />Your business accidentally damages property belonging to a customer.<br /><br />Advertising Injury<br /><br />A business faces certain claims involving advertising or related personal injury allegations covered by the policy.<br /><br />Third Party Accidents<br /><br />A person who is not an employee suffers an injury connected to your business operations.<br /><br />These examples illustrate why general liability is relevant to businesses that interact with customers, visitors, vendors, and other third parties.<br /><br />Examples of Professional Liability Claims<br /><br />Professional liability applies to a different set of risks.<br /><br />Professional Mistakes<br /><br />A client claims that an error in your professional work caused financial losses.<br /><br />Incorrect Advice<br /><br />A client alleges that advice provided by your business caused them financial harm.<br /><br />Missed Deadlines<br /><br />A professional service provider misses an important deadline and the client claims financial damages.<br /><br />Failure to Deliver a Professional Service<br /><br />A client alleges that the business failed to provide the service according to the agreed professional standard.<br /><br />The policy language determines whether a particular claim qualifies for coverage.<br /><br />Do I Need General Liability or Professional Liability?<br /><br />The answer depends on what your business does.<br /><br />A business that works directly with customers or their property may have a strong reason to consider general liability coverage.<br /><br />A business that provides professional advice or specialized services may need professional liability.<br /><br />Some businesses have exposure to both categories.<br /><br />For example, an IT consulting company could face a claim involving damage to a customer’s property as well as a separate claim alleging that professional advice caused financial losses.<br /><br />Carrying both policies can provide broader protection when a business faces different types of liability risks.<br /><br />Which Businesses Commonly Need General Liability Insurance?<br /><br />General liability can be relevant to many types of businesses.<br /><br />Contractors<br /><br />Construction companies<br /><br />Retail stores<br /><br />Restaurants<br /><br />Cleaning companies<br /><br />Landscapers<br /><br />Photographers<br /><br />Event businesses<br /><br />Beauty businesses<br /><br />Repair businesses<br /><br />Professional offices<br /><br />The exact insurance requirements depend on the business, location, contracts, and operations.<br /><br />A contractor working inside customer homes, for example, faces different third party risks from an online consultant who rarely meets clients in person.<br /><br />Which Businesses Commonly Need Professional Liability Insurance?<br /><br />Professional liability can be particularly relevant to businesses that provide advice, specialized knowledge, or professional services.<br /><br />Examples include:<br /><br />Consultants<br /><br />Accountants<br /><br />Architects<br /><br />Engineers<br /><br />Marketing professionals<br /><br />Technology consultants<br /><br />Designers<br /><br />Real estate professionals<br /><br />Financial professionals<br /><br />Legal professionals<br /><br />The terminology can also vary by industry. Professional liability insurance is commonly associated with errors and omissions coverage.<br /><br />Can a Business Have Both Policies?<br /><br />Yes.<br /><br />General liability and professional liability address different categories of risk, so having both can make sense for businesses exposed to both physical and professional risks.<br /><br />Consider an architecture firm.<br /><br />A visitor could suffer an injury at the firm’s office, creating a potential general liability claim.<br /><br />A client could also allege that an error in architectural work caused financial losses, creating a potential professional liability claim.<br /><br />The two policies address different situations.<br /><br />How Much Does General Liability Insurance Cost?<br /><br />The price depends on
