  <h1 style="color: #172554; font-size: 34px; font-weight: 700; line-height: 1.25; margin: 0px 0px 20px;">How Much Does Small Business Insurance Cost in 2027?</h1>

  <p>Running a small business involves risks that can be expensive to handle without the right protection. A customer could get injured at your premises, an employee could suffer a work-related injury, a cyberattack could expose customer information, or a fire could damage your equipment and inventory.</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="Store owner Photos - Download Free High-Quality Pictures | Magnific  (formerly Freepik)" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="426" src="https://img.magnific.com/free-photo/medium-shot-black-woman-running-small-business_23-2150171819.jpg?semt=ais_hybrid&amp;w=740&amp;q=80" style="--xgflle: 1.5010141987829615; max-width: 740px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>Business insurance helps protect companies against some of these financial losses. However, the cost can vary considerably depending on the type of business, location, number of employees, coverage limits and risks involved.</p>

  <p>So, how much should a small business budget for insurance in 2027?</p>

  <p>Recent US pricing research provides useful starting points. Insureon reports median monthly premiums of $45 for general liability, $83 for a business owner's policy and $88 for professional liability. MoneyGeek's September 2026 analysis reports higher modeled averages for several coverage types, illustrating why business owners should compare methodologies rather than rely on one headline figure.</p>

    <p style="color: #1e3a8a; margin: 0px;"><b>Quick answer:</b> Small business insurance can cost anywhere from a few dozen dollars per month for an individual policy to several hundred dollars or more for businesses with multiple coverage needs. As a reference, Insureon's published median premiums include $45 per month for general liability, $83 for a business owner's policy and $88 for professional liability. Your actual 2027 premium will depend on your business and the policy you choose.</p>

  <p>This guide focuses on businesses operating in the United States. The cost figures are reference benchmarks from published 2026 research, not guaranteed 2027 quotes. Always request an individual quote before budgeting for a policy.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 26px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 9px;">How Much Does Each Type of Business Insurance Cost?</h2>

  <p>The best way to estimate your business insurance budget is to understand the individual policies your company may need. Here is what each major type of coverage does and what recent pricing research suggests.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 28px;">1. General Liability Insurance</h3>

  <p><b>Insureon median reference: $45 per month.</b></p>

  <p>General liability insurance protects businesses against certain third-party claims involving bodily injury, property damage and personal or advertising injury.</p>

  <p>For example, imagine a customer visits your shop, slips on a wet floor and suffers an injury. They may claim that your business was responsible. General liability insurance can help cover eligible legal expenses, settlements or judgments, subject to the policy terms and limits.</p>

  <p>This coverage is relevant to many businesses that interact with customers, work at client premises, advertise their services or operate physical locations.</p>

  <p>However, general liability does not cover every business risk. It generally does not replace workers' compensation, professional liability, commercial auto or commercial property insurance.</p>

  <p><b>What affects its cost?</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The type of business and its risk classification.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Annual revenue and number of employees.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Customer foot traffic and the nature of business operations.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Coverage limits and deductible.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Location and claims history.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><a href="https://www.insureon.com/small-business-insurance/cost" rel="noopener" style="color: #2563eb; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">See Insureon's general business insurance cost information</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 28px;">2. Business Owner's Policy (BOP)</h3>

  <p><b>Insureon median reference: $83 per month.</b></p>

  <p>A Business Owner's Policy, commonly called a BOP, combines several types of business protection into one package. A typical BOP includes general liability and commercial property coverage. Business income coverage may also be included, depending on the policy.</p>

  <p>For example, a small retail store could use a BOP to protect against eligible customer injury claims and covered damage to its inventory, equipment or premises.</p>

  <p>Bundling these protections can be more convenient and sometimes less expensive than purchasing separate policies. However, a BOP does not automatically include every type of insurance a business needs.</p>

  <p><b>A BOP may be worth considering if your business:</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Operates from a physical office, shop or other premises.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Owns equipment, furniture, inventory or other business property.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Has customer-facing operations.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Wants general liability and property protection in one package.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Businesses with unusual risks or operations that exceed an insurer's eligibility rules may need separate commercial policies instead.</p>

  <p><a href="https://www.thehartford.com/business-insurance" rel="noopener" style="color: #2563eb; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">Explore The Hartford's small business insurance options</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 28px;">3. Workers' Compensation Insurance</h3>

  <p><b>Insureon median reference: $54 per month.</b></p>

  <p>Workers' compensation insurance helps cover eligible medical expenses, rehabilitation costs and lost wages when an employee suffers a work-related injury or illness.</p>

  <p>For example, if an employee in a warehouse injures their back while lifting stock, workers' compensation may help pay for covered medical treatment and a portion of their lost wages.</p>

  <p>Workers' compensation is particularly important because many US states require employers to carry it. However, the exact requirements differ. Rules may depend on the number of employees, business structure, industry and state-specific exemptions.</p>

  <p>Do not assume that a small business is automatically exempt simply because it has only one or two workers. Check your state's requirements.</p>

  <p><b>What affects the cost?</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Employee payroll and number of workers.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The type of work employees perform.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Workers' compensation classification codes.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Claims history and experience modification factors, where applicable.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">State rules and rating systems.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Construction businesses and other employers with physically demanding or hazardous work may pay substantially more than office-based businesses.</p>

  <p><a href="https://www.insureon.com/small-business-insurance/workers-compensation/cost" rel="noopener" style="color: #2563eb; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">Explore workers' compensation insurance costs</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 28px;">4. Professional Liability Insurance</h3>

  <p><b>Insureon median reference: $88 per month.</b></p>

  <p>Professional liability insurance, also known as errors and omissions insurance or E&amp;O, helps protect businesses against certain claims that their professional services, advice or work caused a client financial harm.</p>

  <p>Imagine a marketing consultant provides a client with a campaign strategy that allegedly causes the client to lose revenue. The client may claim that the consultant made a professional error or failed to deliver the agreed service.</p>

  <p>Professional liability insurance can help cover eligible defence costs and other covered losses, even where the business disputes the allegation.</p>

  <p>This coverage is particularly relevant to consultants, accountants, designers, IT service providers, marketing agencies and other professional service businesses.</p>

  <p>Professional liability policies are often written on a claims-made basis. This means the policy generally needs to be active when the claim is made, subject to the policy's retroactive date and other terms. Some businesses purchase extended reporting period or tail coverage when changing or ending a policy.</p>

  <p><a href="https://www.insureon.com/small-business-insurance/professional-liability/cost" rel="noopener" style="color: #2563eb; text-decoration: underline;" target="_blank">Learn more about professional liability insurance costs</a></p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 28px;">5. Cyber Liability Insurance</h3>

  <p><b>Insureon median reference: $129 per month.</b></p>

  <p>Cyber liability insurance can help businesses manage certain financial consequences of cyber incidents, including data breaches, ransomware attacks and network security failures.</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="Small business cyber security Images - Free Download on Magnific (formerly  Freepik)" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="426" src="https://img.magnific.com/free-photo/diverse-team-working-overtime-criminal-investigation-searching-evidence-arhive-room-private-detectives-working-through-every-clue-crime-scene-analyzing-criminology-report_482257-62937.jpg?semt=ais_hybrid&amp;w=740&amp;q=80" style="--xgflle: 1.5010141987829615; max-width: 740px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>Depending on the policy, coverage may include eligible expenses for investigating a breach, notifying affected customers, restoring systems, responding to ransomware incidents and handling certain third-party claims.</p>

  <p>For example, a small online retailer that stores customer information may suffer a data breach. The business could face costs involving forensic investigation, legal advice, customer notification and recovery.</p>

  <p>Cyber insurance may help cover some of these expenses, but exclusions, security requirements, waiting periods and coverage limits vary considerably.</p>

  <p><b>What affects its cost?</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The volume and sensitivity of data stored.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Annual revenue and business size.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Cybersecurity controls and security practices.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Industry and exposure to cyber threats.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">Selected coverage limits and deductible.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Businesses that process payments, manage customer records or rely heavily on digital systems should consider whether their current protection is sufficient.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 28px;">6. Commercial Property Insurance</h3>

  <p><b>Insureon median reference: $108 per month.</b></p>

  <p>Commercial property insurance helps protect business-owned or insured property against covered physical losses. Depending on the policy, this may include buildings, equipment, furniture, inventory and other business assets.</p>

  <p>For example, if a covered fire damages a small business's office equipment and inventory, commercial property insurance may help pay for eligible repairs or replacement.</p>

  <p>Businesses operating from rented premises may still need property coverage for their own equipment, stock and improvements. A landlord's building policy does not automatically protect a tenant's business belongings.</p>

  <p>Premiums can be influenced by the value of insured property, building construction, location, security measures, deductible and exposure to risks such as fire, theft, storms and flooding.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 28px;">7. Commercial Auto Insurance</h3>

  <p><b>Insureon median reference: $245 per month.</b></p>

  <p>Commercial auto insurance helps protect businesses against covered losses involving vehicles used for business operations. Depending on the policy, it can include liability protection and physical damage coverage for insured vehicles.</p>

  <p>For example, a delivery business may need commercial auto insurance for vehicles used to transport goods. A company may also need coverage when employees drive vehicles for work-related activities.</p>

  <p>The cost can vary widely depending on the type of vehicle, number of vehicles, drivers, driving history, annual mileage, location and business use.</p>

  <p>Commercial auto insurance is especially important for contractors, delivery companies, transportation businesses and other operations that rely on vehicles.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 26px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 9px;">Why Does Small Business Insurance Cost So Much?</h2>

  <p>Two businesses with the same number of employees can receive very different insurance quotes. Insurers assess the risks associated with the company's operations, assets, location and financial profile.</p>

  <p>Here are some of the most important factors that influence premiums.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">1. Industry and business activities</h3>

  <p>The nature of your work is one of the biggest pricing factors. A construction contractor may face a higher risk of employee injuries and third-party property damage than a freelance writer working from home.</p>

  <p>Similarly, a restaurant has different property, customer and operational risks from an accounting consultancy. Insurers use industry classifications and other underwriting information to assess these differences.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">2. Number of employees and payroll</h3>

  <p>As a business hires more workers, its exposure to workplace injuries, employment-related claims and other risks can increase.</p>

  <p>Payroll is particularly important when calculating workers' compensation premiums. Insurers may also consider employee roles, work locations and classification codes.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">3. Business location</h3>

  <p>Insurance costs can vary by state, city and even the specific area where a business operates.</p>

  <p>Factors such as local claim trends, legal environments, weather risks, crime rates, property values and state insurance rules can influence premiums.</p>

  <p>For example, a business located in an area exposed to hurricanes or other severe weather may face different property insurance pricing from a similar business in a lower-risk location.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">4. Annual revenue and business size</h3>

  <p>Revenue can help insurers estimate the scale of business activity and the potential size of certain claims. A company with substantial sales, many customers or complex operations may have greater exposure than a smaller operation.</p>

  <p>However, revenue is only one part of the underwriting process. It does not determine every policy premium on its own.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">5. Coverage limits and deductibles</h3>

  <p>A policy with a higher coverage limit generally costs more than one with a lower limit, all other factors being equal.</p>

  <p>The deductible also matters. Choosing a higher deductible may reduce the premium, but it means your business must pay more out of pocket before the insurer contributes to a covered claim.</p>

  <p>Do not select a deductible simply because it makes the quote cheaper. Make sure the business can comfortably afford it if a claim occurs.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">6. Claims history</h3>

  <p>Previous insurance claims can influence how an insurer evaluates your business. A history of frequent or costly claims may result in higher premiums, stricter terms or difficulty obtaining certain coverage.</p>

  <p>Maintaining good safety practices, cybersecurity controls, employee training and property protection measures can help reduce preventable losses.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 26px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 9px;">How Much Insurance Does a Small Business Actually Need?</h2>

  <p>Buying every available policy is not necessarily the right approach. A better starting point is to identify the risks your business faces, the coverage required by law or contract, and the financial losses you could not comfortably absorb yourself.</p>

  <p>Here are examples of coverage considerations for different business types.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Freelancers and independent consultants</h3>

  <p>A consultant working remotely may have relatively limited property and premises exposure. However, professional liability may be important if clients could claim that advice, missed deadlines or professional work caused financial harm.</p>

  <p>General liability may also be relevant if the consultant visits client premises or meets customers in person. Cyber coverage may be worth considering if the business handles sensitive client information.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Retail shops and small stores</h3>

  <p>A retail business may need general liability for customer injury claims and commercial property protection for stock, equipment and other assets.</p>

  <p>A BOP may combine relevant protections. Businesses with employees should also review workers' compensation requirements in their state.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Construction and contracting businesses</h3>

  <p>Contractors may need general liability, workers' compensation, commercial auto and tools or equipment coverage. Professional liability may also be relevant for businesses that provide design, engineering or consulting services.</p>

  <p>Clients, project owners and licensing authorities may impose specific insurance requirements before a contractor can begin work.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Online businesses and e-commerce stores</h3>

  <p>An online business may not have a physical storefront, but it can still face liability, property and cyber risks.</p>

  <p>General liability may be relevant for product-related claims, while cyber insurance may help address certain costs arising from covered data breaches or cyber incidents. Businesses holding inventory may also need property coverage.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Businesses with employees</h3>

  <p>Employers should investigate workers' compensation requirements in every state where their employees work. They may also need other coverage depending on their operations, employee count, contracts and exposure to employment-related claims.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 26px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 9px;">How to Save Money on Small Business Insurance</h2>

  <p>Insurance is an important business expense, but there are practical ways to manage the cost without leaving major risks unprotected.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Compare quotes from multiple insurers</h3>

  <p>Do not assume the first quote is the best one. Request quotes from several insurers or a licensed independent agent. Compare equivalent coverage limits, deductibles, exclusions and endorsements so you can identify meaningful differences.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Consider bundling eligible coverage</h3>

  <p>A BOP may combine general liability and commercial property coverage in one package. Depending on the insurer and your eligibility, bundling can be more convenient and may cost less than buying separate policies.</p>

  <p>However, check the policy's limits and exclusions. A package is not automatically the most suitable option for every business.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Review coverage limits and deductibles</h3>

  <p>Choose limits that reflect your actual exposure and any contractual requirements. Higher limits may increase the premium, while higher deductibles may reduce it.</p>

  <p>Make sure your business can afford the deductible if a covered claim occurs. Saving a small amount on premiums is not worthwhile if the deductible becomes financially difficult to pay.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Improve workplace safety</h3>

  <p>Employee training, safety procedures, equipment maintenance and proper documentation can reduce preventable workplace incidents. These measures may also help your business maintain a more favorable claims history over time.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Strengthen cybersecurity</h3>

  <p>Use multifactor authentication, keep software updated, maintain secure backups and train employees to recognize phishing attempts. Strong security practices can reduce cyber risk, although insurers may still apply their own underwriting requirements.</p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 21px; margin-top: 25px;">Review your policy every year</h3>

  <p>Your business may have changed since you first purchased insurance. You may have hired employees, increased revenue, purchased equipment, expanded into new locations or started offering different services.</p>

  <p>Review your coverage at renewal so that it reflects your current operations. Avoid paying for protection you no longer need, but do not remove coverage that remains important simply to reduce the premium.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 26px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 9px;">How to Get a Small Business Insurance Quote</h2>

  <p>Getting an accurate quote is easier when you prepare the basic information insurers need to assess your business.</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="Financial planning meeting Photos - Download Free High-Quality Pictures |  Magnific (formerly Freepik)" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="360" src="https://img.magnific.com/free-photo/business-meeting-office_1268-21517.jpg?semt=ais_hybrid&amp;w=740&amp;q=80" style="--xgflle: 1.7788461538461537; max-width: 740px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>Depending on the policy, you may be asked for:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">Business name, address and legal structure.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">Industry and a description of your business activities.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">Annual revenue and estimated payroll.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">Number of employees and their job duties.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">Business property, equipment and inventory values.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">Vehicle details and driver information, if commercial auto coverage is needed.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">Previous insurance claims and existing policies.</li>
    <li style="margin-bottom: 9px;">The coverage limits and deductibles you want.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Once you receive quotes, compare the full policy details rather than choosing based only on the monthly premium.</p>

  <p>Pay particular attention to exclusions, limits, deductibles, endorsements, payment fees and whether the insurer is licensed to provide the coverage in your state.</p>

  <h2 style="border-bottom-color: rgb(219, 234, 254); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 2px; border-bottom: 2px solid rgb(219, 234, 254); color: #172554; font-size: 26px; margin-top: 40px; padding-bottom: 9px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">How much does small business insurance cost per month?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">The cost depends on the coverage you purchase and your business risk. Insureon reports median monthly premiums of $45 for general liability, $83 for a business owner's policy and $88 for professional liability. Businesses that need multiple policies may pay considerably more.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">What is the cheapest type of business insurance?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">In Insureon's published median data, general liability is less expensive than several other common policy types at $45 per month. However, the cheapest policy is not necessarily the most important one for your business. Your coverage needs depend on your operations and risks.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">Is business insurance required by law?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Some types of business insurance are legally required in certain circumstances. Workers' compensation is required for many employers, although state rules and exemptions differ. Commercial auto liability coverage is generally required for vehicles subject to state financial responsibility laws. Other coverage may be required by contracts, landlords or licensing authorities.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">Does an LLC need business insurance?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">An LLC may still need business insurance. Forming an LLC does not automatically protect the business from every lawsuit, employee injury, property loss or cyber incident. The appropriate coverage depends on the company's activities, assets, employees and contractual obligations.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">Can I get business insurance if I work from home?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Yes. Home-based businesses can obtain business insurance. Depending on the work performed, they may need professional liability, general liability, cyber insurance or coverage for business equipment. A standard homeowners policy may not adequately protect business property or business-related liability.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">Does general liability insurance cover employees?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">General liability generally addresses certain third-party claims, not employees' work-related injuries. Workers' compensation is the coverage designed to address eligible workplace injuries and illnesses. Employers should review the applicable state requirements.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">Is a Business Owner's Policy cheaper than buying separate policies?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">A BOP can be more cost-effective because it packages certain coverages, commonly general liability and commercial property. Whether it is cheaper depends on the insurer, the business's eligibility and the coverage selected. Compare the total premium and policy terms.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">Why is business insurance more expensive for contractors?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Contractors may face greater exposure to workplace injuries, property damage and third-party liability claims. Their premiums can also be affected by payroll, subcontractor arrangements, project types, equipment and claims history. The actual price varies by trade and business operations.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-radius: 6px; border: 1px solid rgb(219, 226, 234); margin: 15px 0px; padding: 18px;">
    <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 10px;">How can I lower my small business insurance premium?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">Compare equivalent quotes, consider eligible coverage bundles, maintain good safety practices, strengthen cybersecurity, review deductibles and reassess your coverage at renewal. Avoid reducing essential protection just to obtain a lower premium.</p>
  </div>

  <div style="background: repeat rgb(23, 37, 84); border-radius: 8px; color: white; margin: 25px 0px; padding: 25px 24px;">
    <p style="color: white; font-size: 20px; font-weight: 700; margin: 0px 0px 13px;">There is no universal small business insurance price.</p>
    <p style="color: #e2e8f0; margin: 0px 0px 13px;">A freelancer may need only a few specific policies, while a contractor, retailer or employer may need several types of protection. Your industry, location, employees, revenue, property and coverage limits all influence the final cost.</p>
    <p style="color: #e2e8f0; margin: 0px;">Use published pricing benchmarks to plan your budget, then request individual quotes from insurers or licensed agents. Compare the protection each policy provides, not just the amount you pay every month. The right coverage should address your business's real risks without leaving major gaps.</p>
