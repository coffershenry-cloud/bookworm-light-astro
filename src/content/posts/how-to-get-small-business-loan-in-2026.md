  <p style="color: #596579; font-size: 18px;">

Traditional banks, government-backed lending programmes and online financing platforms all have different eligibility requirements. Review your personal and business credit, prepare your financial documents, compare lenders and submit an application that demonstrates your ability to repay the loan. If you are in the United States, you may also explore financing backed by the Small Business Administration (SBA).

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 38px;">
    What Is a Small Business Loan?
  </h2>

  <p>
    A small business loan is money borrowed by a business from a bank, credit union, government-supported lending programme or private financing company. The business agrees to repay the borrowed amount, usually with interest and sometimes additional fees, according to an agreed repayment schedule.
  </p>

  <p>
    Unlike a business grant, a loan is not free funding. The borrower must repay the money even if the business does not achieve the expected results.
  </p>

  <p>
    Business loans may be provided as a lump sum, a revolving credit facility or financing intended for a specific purchase. The amount available, interest rate, repayment period and eligibility requirements depend on the lender and the type of financing.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 38px;">
    Why Do Small Businesses Apply for Loans?
  </h2>

  <p>
    Businesses borrow money for different reasons. A company may have a profitable opportunity but lack the cash needed to take advantage of it. Another business may need temporary funding to manage expenses while waiting for customers to pay outstanding invoices.
  </p>

  <p>Common reasons for applying for a business loan include:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Business expansion:</b> Opening another location, increasing production capacity or entering a new market.</li>
    <li><b>Equipment purchases:</b> Buying vehicles, machinery, computers or other essential business equipment.</li>
    <li><b>Working capital:</b> Paying rent, suppliers, payroll and other regular operating expenses.</li>
    <li><b>Inventory purchases:</b> Buying additional stock to meet customer demand.</li>
    <li><b>Business acquisition:</b> Purchasing an existing business or buying out a business partner.</li>
    <li><b>Debt refinancing:</b> Replacing existing business debt with financing that may offer more suitable repayment terms.</li>
    <li><b>Property improvements:</b> Purchasing or renovating commercial premises.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Before borrowing, consider whether the money will help the business generate enough income or provide enough operational value to justify the repayment obligation.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 40px;">
    Types of Small Business Loans Available in 2026
  </h2>

  <p>
    There is no single loan product that suits every business. Understanding the main financing options can help you choose one that matches your business needs.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 22px; margin-top: 28px;">
    1. Traditional Bank Business Loans
  </h3>

  <p>
    Traditional business loans are offered by banks and credit unions. They are commonly used by established businesses that need financing for expansion, equipment, property or other significant expenses.
  </p>

  <p>
    Banks generally assess the financial strength of the business and the creditworthiness of its owners. They may review revenue, profitability, existing debts, business history and available collateral.
  </p>

  <p>
    Some bank loans have fixed repayment schedules, while others have variable interest rates. Eligibility can be more demanding than with certain online financing products, especially for businesses without an established operating history.
  </p>

  <p><b>Advantages:</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Potential access to substantial financing.</li>
    <li>Established lending relationships and financial support.</li>
    <li>Repayment terms that may suit longer-term investments.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><b>Limitations:</b></p>
  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Strict eligibility and documentation requirements.</li>
    <li>Approval may take longer than some online alternatives.</li>
    <li>Collateral or personal guarantees may be required.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 22px; margin-top: 28px;">
    2. SBA-Backed Business Loans
  </h3>

  <p>
    The <a href="https://www.sba.gov/" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb; font-weight: bold;" target="_blank">U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA)</a> supports small business financing through programmes that provide guarantees to participating lenders.
  </p>

  <p>
    An important distinction is that the SBA generally does not lend the money directly to borrowers through its standard 7(a) programme. Instead, approved lenders provide the financing, while the SBA guarantees a portion of the loan. This can encourage lenders to provide financing to eligible businesses under the programme's rules.
  </p>

  <p><b>The main SBA loan programmes include:</b></p>

  <p><b>SBA 7(a) loans</b></p>
  <p>
    The 7(a) programme is the SBA's primary business loan programme. Depending on the specific loan and lender, funds may be used for working capital, equipment, eligible real estate purchases, business acquisitions, debt refinancing and other approved business purposes.
  </p>

  <p>
    The maximum loan amount for most 7(a) loans is $5 million. Actual approval, loan terms and interest rates depend on the borrower, lender and applicable programme requirements.
  </p>

  <p><b>SBA 504 loans</b></p>
  <p>
    The 504 programme provides long-term financing for major fixed assets, such as eligible commercial real estate and equipment. It is generally structured through a participating lender and a Certified Development Company (CDC).
  </p>

  <p>
    It is designed for businesses making substantial investments in eligible fixed assets rather than financing ordinary short-term operating expenses.
  </p>

  <p><b>SBA Microloans</b></p>
  <p>
    The Microloan programme provides smaller loans of up to $50,000 through approved intermediary lenders. These loans may help businesses with working capital, inventory, supplies, furniture and equipment.
  </p>

  <p>
    Microloans can be particularly relevant to smaller businesses that do not need the larger amounts available through other financing programmes.
  </p>

  <p>
    <b>Important eligibility note:</b> SBA business financing is intended for qualifying businesses operating in the United States. Applicants must meet the relevant programme and lender requirements. A business outside the United States should not assume it can qualify for an SBA loan.
  </p>

  <p>
    <b>Official information:</b>
    <a href="https://www.sba.gov/loans" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb;" target="_blank">Explore SBA loan programmes</a>.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 22px; margin-top: 28px;">
    3. Online Business Loans
  </h3>

  <p>
    Online business lenders and financing platforms allow businesses to explore funding without necessarily visiting a bank branch. Applications are often completed digitally, and some providers use connected bank account information to review business finances.
  </p>

  <p>
    Online financing can be useful for businesses that need a more convenient application process or do not meet the requirements of a particular traditional bank.
  </p>

  <p>
    However, faster applications do not automatically mean cheaper borrowing. Some online products have higher costs, shorter repayment periods or frequent repayment schedules. Always examine the total repayment amount before accepting an offer.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 22px; margin-top: 28px;">
    4. Secured and Unsecured Business Loans
  </h3>

  <p>
    Business loans can also be classified according to whether the borrower must provide collateral.
  </p>

  <p><b>Secured business loans</b></p>
  <p>
    A secured loan is backed by an asset that the lender can claim under the loan agreement if the borrower fails to meet their obligations. Depending on the financing arrangement, collateral may include equipment, vehicles, property or other business assets.
  </p>

  <p>
    Providing collateral may improve access to financing or influence the loan terms. However, it creates a serious risk: the business could lose the pledged asset if it defaults.
  </p>

  <p><b>Unsecured business loans</b></p>
  <p>
    An unsecured loan does not require a specific asset to be pledged as collateral. The lender instead places greater emphasis on factors such as credit history, revenue, cash flow and repayment capacity.
  </p>

  <p>
    Unsecured does not necessarily mean risk-free. A lender may still require a personal guarantee, meaning an owner could become personally responsible for the debt if the business cannot repay it.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 22px; margin-top: 28px;">
    5. Business Lines of Credit
  </h3>

  <p>
    A business line of credit provides access to a predetermined amount of money that a business can draw from when needed, subject to the agreement and lender approval.
  </p>

  <p>
    Unlike a traditional term loan, which generally provides a lump sum, a revolving line of credit allows the business to borrow, repay and potentially borrow again as credit becomes available.
  </p>

  <p>
    It can be useful for managing seasonal cash flow, purchasing inventory, covering temporary operating expenses or handling unexpected costs.
  </p>

  <p>
    Interest or other borrowing charges generally depend on the amount used and the product's terms. Some facilities also have maintenance, draw or other fees, so review the complete agreement.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #1d4ed8; font-size: 22px; margin-top: 28px;">
    6. Equipment Financing
  </h3>

  <p>
    Equipment financing is designed to help businesses purchase machinery, vehicles, computers and other equipment needed for operations.
  </p>

  <p>
    The equipment being purchased may serve as collateral for the financing. This means the lender may have rights over the asset if the borrower defaults.
  </p>

  <p>
    Equipment financing can be suitable when a business needs a specific asset but prefers to spread the purchase cost over time rather than paying the full amount upfront.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    What Are the Requirements for a Small Business Loan?
  </h2>

  <p>
    Loan requirements vary between lenders and financing products. A traditional bank, an SBA-approved lender and an online financing company may assess the same business differently.
  </p>

  <p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="Documents Needed for Small Business Loan Approval: A Complete Guide" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="322" src="https://cdn.biz2credit.com/images/html5/documents-needed-for-small-business-loan.webp" style="--xgflle: 1.9859484777517564; max-width: 848px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p><p>However, several factors commonly influence business loan decisions.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">1. Personal and Business Credit History</h3>

  <p>
    Lenders often review the personal credit history of business owners, particularly when the business is relatively new or the owner is expected to provide a personal guarantee.
  </p>

  <p>
    A stronger credit history can improve the likelihood of qualifying for certain products and may help a borrower access more favourable terms. A poor credit history does not automatically make every loan impossible, but it can limit the available options.
  </p>

  <p>
    There is no universal minimum credit score for every small business loan. Some online lenders publish minimum score requirements, while banks and SBA lenders consider credit alongside other financial information.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">2. Annual Revenue and Cash Flow</h3>

  <p>
    Lenders want to know whether your business generates enough income to manage its existing expenses and make the proposed loan repayments.
  </p>

  <p>
    They may examine bank statements, tax returns, profit and loss statements, revenue trends and outstanding debts.
  </p>

  <p>
    A business with substantial sales may still struggle to qualify if its expenses are too high or its cash flow is inconsistent. Revenue alone does not prove that a business can afford additional debt.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">3. Time in Business</h3>

  <p>
    Many lenders prefer businesses with an established operating history because they have more financial information to assess.
  </p>

  <p>
    A newer company may find it harder to obtain a conventional bank loan because it has limited revenue history and fewer records demonstrating its ability to repay.
  </p>

  <p>
    However, requirements differ. Some products are designed for younger businesses, while others require one or more years of operating history.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">4. Business Plan and Loan Purpose</h3>

  <p>
    Lenders may ask how you intend to use the borrowed money. A clear explanation helps them assess whether the financing is appropriate and whether the business has a realistic plan for repayment.
  </p>

  <p>
    For example, a business requesting equipment financing should be able to identify the equipment, explain how it will support operations and show how the business expects to manage the repayments.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">5. Collateral and Personal Guarantees</h3>

  <p>
    Some loans require collateral, while others may require a personal guarantee from one or more business owners.
  </p>

  <p>
    Before signing, understand exactly which assets are being pledged and whether you are accepting personal responsibility for the debt.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    Documents You May Need When Applying
  </h2>

  <p>
    Preparing your documents before applying can make the process more organised and help prevent avoidable delays. The exact requirements depend on the lender and type of loan.
  </p>

  <p>Commonly requested documents and information include:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Government-issued identification for business owners.</li>
    <li>Business registration and formation documents.</li>
    <li>Employer Identification Number (EIN), where applicable.</li>
    <li>Business and personal tax returns.</li>
    <li>Recent business bank statements.</li>
    <li>Profit and loss statements.</li>
    <li>Balance sheets and cash flow statements.</li>
    <li>Details of existing business debts and monthly repayments.</li>
    <li>Business plan or explanation of how the funds will be used.</li>
    <li>Ownership information and details of any guarantors.</li>
    <li>Equipment quotes, purchase agreements or other supporting documents when relevant.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Some online lenders use digital bank connections to verify financial information. Others may ask applicants to upload statements and supporting documents manually.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    How Lenders Evaluate Business Loan Applications
  </h2>

  <p>
    A lender does not simply look at how much money a business earns. It also considers the risks associated with lending to that business.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img alt="Loan Business Calculator: A Mandate for Every Entrepreneur" class="sFlh5c FyHeAf uT7N3d" height="322" src="https://cdn.biz2credit.com/images/html5/loan-business-calculator.webp" style="--xgflle: 1.9859484777517564; max-width: 848px;" width="640" />&nbsp;</p>

  <p>
    One common framework is known as the <b>Five Cs of Credit</b>.
  </p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Character:</b> The borrower's credit history and record of managing financial obligations.</li>
    <li><b>Capacity:</b> The business's ability to generate enough cash to repay the loan.</li>
    <li><b>Capital:</b> The financial resources and investment the owners have committed to the business.</li>
    <li><b>Collateral:</b> Assets that may secure the loan.</li>
    <li><b>Conditions:</b> The purpose of the financing and relevant economic, industry and business circumstances.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    These factors help lenders assess whether the proposed financing is suitable and whether the borrower is likely to repay it. Different institutions may use additional criteria or scoring systems.
  </p>

  <p>
    <a href="https://business.bankofamerica.com/en/resources/factors-that-impact-loan-decisions-and-how-to-increase-your-approval-odds" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb;" target="_blank">Bank of America explains several factors lenders consider</a> when reviewing business financing applications.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    Small Business Loan Interest Rates in 2026
  </h2>

  <p>
    Interest rates are among the most important factors to consider before accepting a business loan. They determine how much it costs to borrow money, in addition to any fees included in the agreement.
  </p>

  <p>
    There is no single interest rate for all small business loans in 2026. The rate offered depends on the lender, loan product, loan amount, repayment period, credit profile, business finances and broader market conditions.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">How SBA 7(a) interest rates work</h3>

  <p>
    SBA 7(a) interest rates are negotiated between the borrower and the participating lender, but they are subject to SBA maximums. Depending on the loan, the rate may be fixed or variable.
  </p>

  <p>
    For variable-rate 7(a) loans, the SBA sets maximum spreads over an approved base rate. The maximum spread depends on the loan amount:
  </p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Loans of $50,000 or less: up to 6.5 percentage points above the base rate.</li>
    <li>Loans above $50,000 and up to $250,000: up to 6 percentage points above the base rate.</li>
    <li>Loans above $250,000 and up to $350,000: up to 4.5 percentage points above the base rate.</li>
    <li>Loans above $350,000: up to 3 percentage points above the base rate.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    These are maximum permitted spreads for variable-rate loans, not guaranteed rates or a promise that a lender will offer the maximum. The base rate and applicable rules matter, and fixed-rate loans follow separate limits.
  </p>

  <p>
    Read the SBA's <a href="https://www.sba.gov/sba-lenders/" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #2563eb;" target="_blank">lender programme information</a> and ask the participating lender for the actual rate offered to your business.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">What other costs should you consider?</h3>

  <p>
    The interest rate is not the only cost of borrowing. Depending on the product, you may also encounter:
  </p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li>Origination or application fees.</li>
    <li>Loan guarantee fees where applicable.</li>
    <li>Late payment charges.</li>
    <li>Annual or account maintenance fees.</li>
    <li>Collateral valuation or closing costs.</li>
    <li>Prepayment penalties on certain products.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    A loan with a lower advertised interest rate is not automatically the cheapest option. Compare the total repayment amount and all mandatory charges before deciding.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    How to Compare Small Business Loan Offers
  </h2>

  <p>
    Before accepting financing, compare the full terms of each offer. Looking only at the amount a lender is willing to provide can lead to an expensive borrowing decision.
  </p>

  <p>Pay particular attention to these details:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Annual percentage rate (APR):</b> Where available, review the APR because it can help reflect the cost of borrowing, including certain fees.</li>
    <li><b>Total repayment:</b> Calculate how much you will repay over the full loan period.</li>
    <li><b>Repayment frequency:</b> Check whether payments are weekly, biweekly or monthly.</li>
    <li><b>Loan term:</b> Understand how long you have to repay the money.</li>
    <li><b>Collateral:</b> Identify assets that could be at risk if you default.</li>
    <li><b>Personal guarantee:</b> Find out whether you are personally responsible for repayment.</li>
    <li><b>Prepayment rules:</b> Check whether paying off the loan early reduces the cost or triggers a penalty.</li>
    <li><b>Variable rates:</b> Understand whether your interest rate and payments can increase.</li>
  </ul>

  <div style="background: repeat rgb(248, 250, 252); border-radius: 8px; border: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); margin: 25px 0px; padding: 20px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; margin-top: 0px;">A simple borrowing example</h3>
    <p>
      Imagine your business borrows $20,000 to purchase equipment. One lender offers a longer repayment period with a lower monthly payment, while another offers a shorter repayment period with higher monthly payments.
    </p>
    <p>
      The lower monthly payment may appear more affordable, but the longer loan could result in more interest being paid over time. You should compare the total repayment amount, not just the monthly instalment.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 0px;">
      This is why it is important to request a complete repayment schedule before signing a loan agreement.
    </p>
  </div>

    Where Can You Apply for a Small Business Loan?

Rather than operating like a single traditional bank, it helps businesses explore financing options from a network of lending partners.

These conditions should not be treated as universal requirements for every Bank of America financing option.

    Businesses considering a bank loan should check the specific product's eligibility requirements and speak with the bank about the available terms.

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    How to Get a Small Business Loan: Step-by-Step Application Process
  </h2>

  <p>
    Once you understand the available financing options, the next step is preparing your application. Following a structured process can help you avoid unnecessary applications and identify a loan that fits your business.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 1: Determine how much money you need</h3>

  <p>
    Start by calculating the amount required to achieve your business objective. Avoid borrowing more than necessary simply because a lender offers a higher amount.
  </p>

  <p>
    Include the purchase price, installation costs, working capital needs and any additional expenses associated with the project.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 2: Identify the right type of financing</h3>

  <p>
    Match the loan to the purpose. Equipment financing may be appropriate for purchasing machinery, while a business line of credit may be more suitable for short-term cash flow requirements.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 3: Review your credit and financial records</h3>

  <p>
    Review your personal and business credit information where applicable. Check for inaccurate details, outstanding debts and other issues that could affect your application.
  </p>

  <p>
    Also examine your financial statements and bank records to ensure that the information is accurate and up to date.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 4: Prepare your documents</h3>

  <p>
    Gather the documents the lender is likely to request. Having tax returns, bank statements, financial statements and business registration information ready can make the process more efficient.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 5: Research and compare lenders</h3>

  <p>
    Compare traditional banks, SBA-backed financing options and online lenders. Look beyond advertised rates and examine eligibility, fees, repayment frequency, loan terms and collateral requirements.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 6: Submit your application</h3>

  <p>
    Complete the application accurately and provide all requested information. Make sure that your revenue figures, loan purpose and business details are consistent across the documents you submit.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 7: Review the loan offer carefully</h3>

  <p>
    If approved, read the complete agreement before accepting. Confirm the amount you will receive, the repayment schedule, interest rate, fees, collateral requirements and any personal guarantee.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color: #334155; font-size: 21px;">Step 8: Use the funds responsibly</h3>

  <p>
    Once the funds are disbursed, use them for the purpose described in your application and monitor the impact on your business finances.
  </p>

  <p>
    Keep track of repayment dates and maintain enough cash flow to meet your obligations.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    Can You Get a Business Loan for a Startup With No Revenue?
  </h2>

  <p>
    Getting a loan for a startup without established revenue can be difficult. Traditional lenders often prefer businesses with a financial track record because it gives them evidence of the company's ability to repay.
  </p>

  <p>
    However, a new business may still have financing options. The most appropriate route depends on the owner's financial position, business plan, available assets and the lender's eligibility rules.
  </p>

  <p>Potential options to investigate include:</p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Microloans:</b> Smaller loans from eligible intermediary lenders may be more appropriate for businesses with modest funding needs.</li>
    <li><b>Equipment financing:</b> A lender may consider financing tied to a specific asset rather than general working capital.</li>
    <li><b>Personal financing:</b> Some entrepreneurs use personal funds or personal borrowing, but this can put their personal finances at risk.</li>
    <li><b>Business grants:</b> Eligible grant programmes may provide funding that does not require repayment, although they often have specific application and eligibility rules.</li>
    <li><b>Friends and family investment:</b> Funding from people you know may be possible, but agreements should be documented clearly to avoid misunderstandings.</li>
    <li><b>Equity investment:</b> Some startups seek investors in exchange for an ownership stake rather than taking on debt.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    A startup should not assume that a business loan is the only way to obtain funding. Compare debt financing with other options and consider the financial risks before committing.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    Why Are Small Business Loan Applications Rejected?
  </h2>

  <p>
    A loan application can be rejected for several reasons. Understanding common problems can help you address weaknesses before applying again.
  </p>

  <ul style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Poor credit history:</b> Missed payments, defaults or excessive existing debt may concern lenders.</li>
    <li><b>Insufficient revenue:</b> The business may not generate enough income to support the proposed repayments.</li>
    <li><b>Limited operating history:</b> A newly established company may not have enough financial records.</li>
    <li><b>Incomplete documentation:</b> Missing or inconsistent information can delay or undermine an application.</li>
    <li><b>Unclear loan purpose:</b> Lenders may be concerned if the business cannot explain how the money will be used.</li>
    <li><b>Insufficient collateral:</b> Some secured products require assets that the applicant cannot provide.</li>
    <li><b>High existing debt:</b> Current financial obligations may leave insufficient room for another loan payment.</li>
    <li><b>Industry or eligibility restrictions:</b> The business may not meet the lender's or programme's rules.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    If your application is rejected, ask the lender for the reason where possible. Review the issue and consider whether a different financing product or a later application would be more appropriate.
  </p>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    How to Improve Your Chances of Getting Approved
  </h2>

  <p>
    Although no strategy can guarantee approval, preparing properly can help you present a stronger application.
  </p>

  <ol style="padding-left: 24px;">
    <li><b>Maintain accurate financial records.</b> Keep your bank statements, tax documents and financial reports organised.</li>
    <li><b>Manage existing debt.</b> Review your current obligations and avoid taking on more borrowing than your business can comfortably support.</li>
    <li><b>Monitor your credit.</b> Check your credit reports and address errors or overdue accounts where possible.</li>
    <li><b>Build a clear business plan.</b> Explain what the funds will achieve and how the business expects to repay the loan.</li>
    <li><b>Request a realistic amount.</b> Borrow according to your actual business needs and repayment capacity.</li>
    <li><b>Compare lenders before applying.</b> Focus on products whose eligibility criteria match your circumstances.</li>
    <li><b>Understand the agreement.</b> Make sure you know the full cost and risks before accepting financing.</li>
  </ol>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    Frequently Asked Questions About Small Business Loans
  </h2>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">What credit score do I need to get a small business loan?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      There is no universal minimum credit score for every business loan. Requirements vary by lender and product. Some online financing products publish minimum personal credit score requirements, while traditional banks may assess credit alongside revenue, cash flow, business history and other factors.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Can I get a small business loan with no revenue?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      It can be difficult because many lenders want evidence that the business can repay the debt. However, some startups may explore microloans, equipment financing, personal funding, grants or investment. Eligibility depends on the specific programme and the applicant's circumstances.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">How much can a small business borrow?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      The amount depends on the lender, financing product, business finances and ability to repay. Some microloan programmes provide smaller amounts, while certain SBA 7(a) loans can reach $5 million. Approval is never guaranteed simply because a programme has a high maximum loan limit.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Are SBA loans easier to get than traditional bank loans?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      SBA guarantees can make certain loans more accessible to eligible small businesses, but applicants must still meet programme and lender requirements. The application process can involve detailed financial reviews and documentation.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">How long does it take to get a business loan?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      Processing time varies. Some online financing providers may make decisions quickly, while traditional bank and SBA-backed applications can take longer because of documentation, underwriting and approval procedures. Ask the lender for its expected timeline.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Is collateral required for every business loan?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      No. Some loans are unsecured, while others require collateral. Even when specific collateral is not required, a lender may ask for a personal guarantee. Review the agreement to understand your responsibilities.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Can I use a business loan to pay existing business debt?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      Some financing products allow eligible business debt refinancing. However, lenders may have specific requirements about the type of debt, the reason for refinancing and whether the new arrangement improves the business's financial position.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div style="border-bottom-color: rgb(226, 232, 240); border-bottom-style: solid; border-bottom-width: 1px; border-bottom: 1px solid rgb(226, 232, 240); padding: 17px 0px;">
    <h3 style="color: #172554; font-size: 19px; margin: 0px 0px 8px;">Is a business loan better than a business line of credit?</h3>
    <p style="margin: 0px;">
      It depends on what the business needs. A term loan provides a lump sum that is repaid according to an agreed schedule, while a line of credit offers flexible access to funds up to an approved limit. Compare costs and repayment terms before choosing.
    </p>
  </div>

  <h2 style="color: #172554; font-size: 27px; margin-top: 42px;">
    Final Thoughts: Is a Small Business Loan Right for You?
  </h2>

  <p>
    A small business loan can provide useful funding when a business needs to expand, purchase equipment, manage cash flow or take advantage of a growth opportunity. However, borrowing should be based on a clear financial need and a realistic repayment plan.
  </p>

  <p>
    Start by identifying the amount you need, researching the available loan types and reviewing your business finances. Compare traditional banks, SBA-backed programmes and online financing providers carefully. Pay attention to interest rates, fees, repayment schedules, collateral and personal guarantees.
  </p>

  <p>
    If you are a startup without established revenue, consider whether a microloan, equipment financing, grant or investment may be more suitable than taking on a conventional business loan.
  </p>

  <p>
    Ultimately, the right financing is not simply the loan that offers the largest amount. It is the option that meets your business needs while keeping repayment obligations manageable.
  </p>
