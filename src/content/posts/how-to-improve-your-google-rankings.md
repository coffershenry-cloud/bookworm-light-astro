<p>That shift has created a new concern for marketers: how does a website become visible when a customer receives an AI-generated answer instead of a conventional list of search results?</p>

<p>New terminology and optimization services have quickly emerged around this question. Generative engine optimization, answer engine optimization and AI visibility are now common parts of the SEO conversation.</p>

<p>These developments deserve attention. Businesses have good reasons to understand how their brands appear in AI-generated recommendations and which websites are being cited as sources.</p>

<p>However, there is a point that can easily get lost in the excitement: <strong>AI search makes strong SEO fundamentals more important, not less.</strong></p>

<p>Websites still need accessible pages, useful information, clear organization, relevant content and credible authority. Businesses still need to understand their customers' questions and create pages that satisfy those needs.</p>

<p>The interface may be changing, but the underlying work of making a website discoverable and useful has not disappeared.</p>

<p style="color:#183b63;font-size:18px;font-weight:bold;margin:0 0 10px;">The quick answer</p>
<p style="margin:0;">Businesses should not abandon traditional SEO to chase AI visibility. They should build a technically accessible website, research genuine search demand, publish useful content, earn relevant authority and monitor traditional search performance. AI visibility tracking can then provide an additional way to understand how the brand appears in generative search experiences.</p>

<h2 id="seo-new-tactics" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">The Temptation to Chase Every New SEO Tactic</h2>

<p>Every major change in search creates an opportunity for marketers to rethink their strategies.</p>

<p>That can be healthy when it leads to better research, experimentation and decision-making. It becomes a problem when every new development receives more attention than the work already producing results.</p>

<p>AI search has created exactly this challenge.</p>

<p>Marketers are now asking questions such as:</p>

<ul style="margin:12px 0 20px;padding-left:24px;">
<li>Should a website create an <code>llms.txt</code> file?</li>
<li>Should existing articles be rewritten specifically for language models?</li>
<li>Should businesses create a separate AI optimization strategy?</li>
<li>Should traditional SEO and AI visibility be treated as completely different marketing channels?</li>
</ul>

<p>These are reasonable questions. The mistake is assuming that every new tactic is automatically necessary or proven to deliver better visibility.</p>

<p>Google's published guidance is particularly useful here. Its documentation for AI features in Search says that established SEO best practices remain relevant. It also says there are no additional technical requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond the normal eligibility requirements for Google Search.</p>

<p>That does not mean every AI platform works exactly like Google. It does mean website owners should be cautious about advice that presents a new optimization trick as a universal requirement.</p>

<div style="background:#f3f6fa;border-left:4px solid #2463a6;border-radius:7px;margin:24px 0;padding:18px 20px;">
<p style="font-weight:bold;margin:0 0 7px;color:#183b63;">A practical rule</p>
<p style="margin:0;">Test new SEO tactics when there is a clear reason to do so. Measure their results, compare them with alternatives and invest more heavily in activities that demonstrate real value. Do not replace a functioning strategy simply because a new acronym has become popular.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="traditional-seo" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">Traditional SEO Still Provides the Foundation</h2>

<p>Consider what needs to happen before a search system can use information from a website.</p>

<p>The information must exist. The relevant page must be accessible. Important content needs to be discoverable. The page should communicate its subject clearly and provide information that is useful for the question being asked.</p>

<p>Those requirements do not disappear when the final interface becomes conversational.</p>

<p>Google's guidance on AI features explains that pages must still meet the technical requirements for Google Search and be eligible to appear with a snippet. Its recommendations continue to emphasize crawlability, internal linking, useful content and a clear website structure.</p>

<p>In other words, the fundamentals remain part of the process.</p>

<h2 id="technical-seo" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">Technical SEO Can Undermine Everything Else</h2>

<p>One of the least glamorous parts of SEO is also one of the easiest to overlook: technical maintenance.</p>

<p>A business can invest considerable time and money in publishing articles and building backlinks, only to discover that important pages have indexing problems, broken links, poor internal linking or other technical issues.</p>

<p>That is why technical checks should be part of an ongoing SEO process rather than something a website owner remembers only when traffic falls.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">What should a technical SEO audit examine?</h3>

<ul style="margin:12px 0 20px;padding-left:24px;">
<li>Whether important pages can be crawled and indexed.</li>
<li>Whether internal links lead to working pages.</li>
<li>Whether duplicate or competing URLs create confusion.</li>
<li>Whether HTTPS is correctly implemented.</li>
<li>Whether page titles and other important elements are properly configured.</li>
<li>Whether the website works well on mobile devices.</li>
<li>Whether relevant AI crawlers can access pages where that access is intended.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="search-intent" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">Search Intent Matters More Than Simply Finding a Keyword</h2>

<p>Another SEO fundamental becomes even more important as search becomes conversational: understanding what the person actually wants.</p>

<p>Imagine someone searches for:</p>

<div style="background:#f3f6fa;border-radius:7px;margin:15px 0;padding:15px 18px;">
<p style="color:#183b63;font-size:17px;font-weight:bold;margin:0;">Best accounting software for freelancers</p>
</div>

<p>That person probably does not want a dictionary definition of accounting software. They may want a comparison of products, pricing information, features, limitations and recommendations that help them decide which platform to use.</p>

<p>Now compare that with:</p>

<div style="background:#f3f6fa;border-radius:7px;margin:15px 0;padding:15px 18px;">
<p style="color:#183b63;font-size:17px;font-weight:bold;margin:0;">What is accounting software?</p>
</div>

<p>The intent is different. The second search is more likely to come from someone looking for an explanation rather than an immediate product comparison.</p>

<p>A page that understands this difference has a better chance of satisfying the reader than one that simply repeats a keyword dozens of times.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">What good keyword research should establish</h3>

<ul style="margin:12px 0 20px;padding-left:24px;">
<li>What people are actually searching for.</li>
<li>Whether the search is informational, commercial or transactional.</li>
<li>How competitive the results appear to be.</li>
<li>What type of page currently satisfies the search.</li>
<li>Whether the topic has a meaningful connection to the business.</li>
<li>Whether an existing page already covers the same search need.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="content-quality" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">More Content Is Not Automatically Better Content</h2>

<p>The pressure to publish constantly can create another problem.</p>

<p>A website discovers a keyword and immediately creates an article. Then it discovers another variation and creates another article. Eventually, the site has hundreds of pages that overlap, repeat the same information and compete with one another.</p>

<p>A stronger content strategy starts with the audience rather than the publishing calendar.</p>

<p>Before creating another page, ask:</p>

<ul style="margin:12px 0 20px;padding-left:24px;">
<li>What questions are customers asking?</li>
<li>Which topics affect their buying decisions?</li>
<li>Which pages already perform well?</li>
<li>Where is useful information missing?</li>
<li>Could an existing page provide a much better answer?</li>
<li>Does this new topic satisfy a search intent that is genuinely different?</li>
</ul>

<p>These questions lead to a more useful content system than publishing articles simply because a keyword tool has generated another suggestion.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">Create something worth discovering</h3>

<p>Google's guidance for AI search emphasizes useful, reliable and people-first content. It also encourages website owners to provide information that adds value rather than simply repeating what is already widely available.</p>

<p>For a small business website, that could mean:</p>

<ul style="margin:12px 0 20px;padding-left:24px;">
<li>Explaining a complicated process with clear practical steps.</li>
<li>Providing an accurate comparison of relevant products.</li>
<li>Adding original research or useful examples.</li>
<li>Explaining important limitations instead of presenting only benefits.</li>
<li>Updating outdated information when the facts change.</li>
<li>Answering the question directly before introducing unnecessary background.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="backlinks" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">Backlinks Still Belong in the Conversation</h2>

<p>A backlink is a link from one website to another. Links can help search engines discover pages and understand relationships between websites.</p>

<p>However, that does not mean every backlink is valuable.</p>

<p>A website should not build a large collection of irrelevant links simply to increase a number in a report.</p>

<p>The more useful question is whether another website has a genuine reason to reference the page.</p>

<p>Original research can earn links. Useful statistics can earn links. Detailed resources can earn links. Strong industry analysis can earn links. A genuinely helpful guide can earn links.</p>

<p>That is very different from treating link building as a numbers game.</p>

<h2 id="ai-visibility" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">AI Visibility Creates a New Measurement Problem</h2>

<p>There is one important question that traditional ranking reports cannot fully answer:</p>

<p><strong>What happens when a customer stops looking at traditional search results and asks an AI system for an answer?</strong></p>

<p>A brand might perform well in conventional search results and still have limited visibility in AI-generated responses for certain questions.</p>

<p>That creates a different measurement challenge.</p>

<p>Marketers need to understand not only whether their pages appear in traditional search results, but also whether their brand is mentioned, recommended or cited when relevant questions are asked through AI platforms.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">What is AI visibility?</h3>

<p>AI visibility describes how a brand or website appears within AI-generated answers. Depending on the platform and measurement method, this may include brand mentions, recommendations, cited pages and links to the website.</p>

<p>It is not exactly the same as a traditional Google ranking. An AI-generated answer may mention a company without linking to its website, or cite a page without presenting it as a direct product recommendation.</p>

<p>That is why marketers should look at several measurements rather than treating one visibility score as the complete picture.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">What This Looks Like for a Real Website</h2>

<p>Imagine an online retailer that has noticed competitors appearing for several valuable commercial searches while its own website receives little organic traffic.</p>

<p>The first response should not necessarily be to publish an article specifically designed for AI search.</p>

<p>Start by examining the website itself.</p>

<p>Are important product pages indexable? Are there broken links? Are duplicate pages competing with one another? Are the main commercial pages targeting relevant searches? Are related pages connected through useful internal links?</p>

<p>Next, examine the competition. Which websites are ranking for the important searches? What information do their pages provide? Are there gaps in their comparisons, pricing explanations or product coverage?</p>

<p>Once those questions have been answered, the retailer can prioritize improvements to the pages that matter most.</p>

<p>After making those changes, it can monitor traditional rankings and relevant AI visibility measurements to see what happens.</p>

<p>This process may sound less exciting than chasing a new optimization trick every month. It is also easier to evaluate because each activity has a clear purpose.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">The Boring SEO Strategy Can Be the Useful One</h2>

<p>Technical SEO, keyword research, content improvements and link building rarely make exciting headlines.</p>

<p>They require research, maintenance and patience. They also rarely produce dramatic results overnight.</p>

<p>But businesses do not need their SEO strategy to be exciting. They need it to be useful.</p>

<p>A technical problem fixed today can prevent future issues. A well-researched commercial page can continue attracting relevant visitors long after publication. A genuinely useful resource can earn references from other websites. A sensible internal linking structure can help readers discover related information.</p>

<p>And a properly researched keyword strategy can prevent a business from spending months creating content that does not satisfy a real search need.</p>

<p>These activities may not have the appeal of a new acronym, but they are measurable and repeatable.</p>

<h2 style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">How to Build an SEO Workflow Around Evidence</h2>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">1. Find the Technical Problems</h3>
<p>Run a site audit and identify issues affecting crawling, indexing, page structure, mobile usability and other technical elements. Prioritize problems according to their likely impact rather than trying to fix every warning at once.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">2. Find the Opportunities</h3>
<p>Research the searches that matter to your customers. Examine search intent, competition, existing rankings and the types of pages already performing in your market.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">3. Improve the Important Pages</h3>
<p>Prioritize pages that can generate meaningful value for the business. Improve their usefulness, structure, accuracy and ability to satisfy the searcher's actual question.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">4. Build Legitimate Authority</h3>
<p>Develop content and relationships that can earn relevant references from other websites. Study competitors to understand where their backlinks come from and where genuine opportunities may exist.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">5. Measure Traditional Search Performance</h3>
<p>Track rankings, organic traffic, backlinks and technical health. Compare performance over time and investigate meaningful changes rather than reacting to every short-term fluctuation.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">6. Monitor AI Visibility Where It Matters</h3>
<p>If customers are using AI platforms to research products or services in your market, monitor brand mentions, citations and competitor visibility. Use the findings to identify content gaps and opportunities worth investigating.</p>

<p>This gives marketers something more valuable than a collection of disconnected tactics. It gives them a system they can evaluate and improve.</p>

<h2 id="seo-faq" style="border-bottom:2px solid #e5ebf2;color:#183b63;font-size:25px;line-height:1.35;margin:40px 0 18px;padding-bottom:9px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">Is traditional SEO still relevant with AI search?</h3>
<p>Yes. AI search changes how information may be presented, but websites still need accessible, useful and relevant information. Traditional SEO activities such as technical optimization, keyword research, content development and link analysis remain important parts of managing search visibility.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">Should businesses stop focusing on Google and focus only on AI search?</h3>
<p>No. Businesses do not need to treat traditional search and AI visibility as mutually exclusive. They can continue improving their search performance while monitoring how their brands and content appear in relevant AI-generated answers.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">What is the difference between SEO and AI visibility?</h3>
<p>Traditional SEO focuses on how websites perform in search results, including rankings, organic traffic and technical health. AI visibility focuses on how brands and their content appear in AI-generated answers, including mentions and citations. The measurements are related, but they are not interchangeable.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">Does Google require an llms.txt file for AI search visibility?</h3>
<p>Google's published guidance says websites do not need to create new machine-readable files, AI text files or special markup to appear in Google AI features. Other AI platforms may have their own documentation and crawler policies, so website owners should check the requirements of the services they care about rather than assume one file is universally necessary.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">What does Semrush's SEO Toolkit do?</h3>
<p>Semrush's SEO Toolkit provides tools for keyword research, competitor analysis, backlink research, rank tracking and technical website auditing. It helps marketers investigate opportunities, identify website issues and monitor search performance.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">What does Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit measure?</h3>
<p>The AI Visibility Toolkit provides measurements and reports related to brand visibility in AI-generated answers. These include mentions, citations, cited pages, competitor comparisons and topic opportunities across supported AI search experiences.</p>

<h3 style="color:#245b91;font-size:19px;margin:25px 0 10px;">Can AI visibility tools guarantee that a brand will appear in AI answers?</h3>
<p>No. Monitoring tools can help businesses understand current visibility and identify opportunities, but they cannot guarantee that an AI platform will mention, recommend or cite a particular brand in future responses.</p>

<p>The search industry is going through a significant change. People are asking questions differently, search engines are generating more answers directly and AI platforms are becoming part of the research process.</p>

<p>The strongest response to AI search may therefore be less dramatic than many marketers expect.</p>
