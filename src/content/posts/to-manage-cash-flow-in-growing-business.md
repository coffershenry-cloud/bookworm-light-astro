Suppliers are sending invoices. Employees need to be paid. Software subscriptions renew every month. New opportunities require money before they start generating revenue.<br /><br />This is where cash flow becomes one of the most important parts of running a business.<br /><br />Revenue tells you how much money your business is generating. Cash flow helps you understand how much money is actually available to run the business right now.<br /><br />A business can generate strong sales and still need to carefully manage when money comes in and when payments go out.<br /><br />Here is how to build a cash flow system that supports a growing business.<br /><br />Know Exactly Where Your Money Is Going<br /><br />The first step is having a clear picture of the money moving through your business.<br /><br />This includes customer payments, invoices, payroll, supplier payments, rent, subscriptions, marketing costs, taxes, and every other expense involved in keeping the business running.<br /><br />As a business grows, these transactions can quickly become difficult to follow.<br /><br />A simple cash flow review can answer a few important questions:<br /><br />How much cash is currently available?<br /><br />Which customers still have outstanding invoices?<br /><br />Which bills are coming up soon?<br /><br />How much money is expected to arrive this month?<br /><br />How much will the business spend during the same period?<br /><br />Having these numbers available makes financial decisions much easier.<br /><br />Keep a Close Eye on Customer Payments<br /><br />Sales only become available cash when customers complete their payments.<br /><br />Businesses that regularly issue invoices can benefit from tracking payment dates and outstanding balances closely.<br /><br />A clear invoicing process usually starts with sending invoices promptly after completing a project or delivering a product or service. Payment terms should also be easy for customers to understand.<br /><br />As the number of customers increases, keeping track of every invoice manually can take a significant amount of time.<br /><br />Financial platforms can help businesses organize accounts receivable and monitor payment activity from a central location. BILL, for example, provides tools designed to help businesses manage invoices and payments as their financial operations grow.<br /><br />The main goal is simple. The business should always have a clear view of money that is expected to arrive.<br /><br />Plan Supplier and Vendor Payments Carefully<br /><br />Every growing business eventually develops relationships with more suppliers, vendors, contractors, and service providers.<br /><br />Each of those relationships can come with invoices and payment deadlines.<br /><br />A structured payment process helps businesses see upcoming obligations before they become urgent.<br /><br />For example, a business may have several invoices due throughout the month. Seeing all of those payments together allows the owner or finance team to plan around the company’s available cash.<br /><br />Businesses handling a large volume of bills may also use financial management platforms such as BILL to organize accounts payable, payment approvals, and vendor transactions.<br /><br />This becomes increasingly valuable as more people become involved in the company’s financial operations.<br /><br />Create a Simple Cash Flow Forecast<br /><br />A cash flow forecast helps you look ahead instead of focusing only on the money currently sitting in the bank account.<br /><br />The process can be relatively simple.<br /><br />Start with the cash currently available. Then estimate the money expected to come in and subtract the payments expected to go out during a specific period.<br /><br />For example, imagine your business currently has $20,000 available.<br /><br />You expect to receive $30,000 from customers over the next month.<br /><br />During the same month, you expect to spend $25,000 on payroll, suppliers, rent, and other expenses.<br /><br />Your forecast gives you an estimated picture of where the business may stand at the end of that period.<br /><br />Updating this regularly can help business owners prepare for upcoming expenses and make better decisions about spending.<br /><br />Build a System for Approving Expenses<br /><br />Growing businesses often involve several people making financial decisions.<br /><br />Employees may submit expenses. Department managers may approve purchases. Business owners or finance teams may authorize payments.<br /><br />A clear approval process keeps these activities organized.<br /><br />The process can vary depending on the size of the company, but the important part is knowing who is responsible for reviewing and approving each expense.<br /><br />Financial software can make these workflows easier to manage, especially when a business is handling a large number of payments each month.<br /><br />A structured approval system also creates better visibility into how money moves through the organization.<br /><br />Review Your Recurring Expenses<br /><br />Monthly subscriptions can quietly become a significant part of business spending.<br /><br />Software tools, marketing platforms, storage services, communication tools, and other subscriptions may all support daily operations.<br /><br />Reviewing these expenses regularly helps you understand exactly what the business is paying for each month.<br /><br />This is also a good opportunity to look at whether each service still supports the company’s current goals.<br /><br />As businesses grow, their needs change. A tool that made sense six months ago may have been replaced by a more useful platform or become unnecessary.<br /><br />Regular reviews keep financial operations organized and help business owners make more informed spending decisions.<br /><br />Keep Some Cash Available for Opportunities<br /><br />Growth often creates opportunities that require quick decisions.<br /><br />A business may have the chance to purchase inventory at a better price, launch a profitable marketing campaign, hire a valuable employee, or invest in equipment that improves operations.<br /><br />Having cash available gives the business more flexibility when opportunities appear.<br /><br />A cash reserve can also provide breathing room when expenses increase or revenue takes longer to arrive than expected.<br /><br />The amount a business keeps in reserve will depend on its industry, operating costs, and revenue patterns. The important part is building cash availability into the company’s financial planning.<br /><br />Use Software That Fits the Size of Your Business<br /><br />The financial tools used by a freelancer will usually look different from those used by a growing company with multiple employees and vendors.<br /><br />The best system depends on how the business operates.<br /><br />Some companies mainly need invoicing tools. Others need help organizing supplier payments, approvals, expenses, and accounts receivable.<br /><br />When choosing financial software, consider questions such as:<br /><br />How many invoices does the business process each month?<br /><br />How many suppliers or vendors require payment?<br /><br />Who approves business expenses?<br /><br />Does the software work with the company’s accounting system?<br /><br />How much of the payment process can be automated?<br /><br />Platforms such as BILL can be useful for businesses that want to bring several financial workflows into a more organized system.<br /><br />The right tool should make financial management easier as the company grows.<br /><br />Review Your Cash Flow Every Week<br /><br />Cash flow works best when it becomes part of the regular business routine.<br /><br />A weekly review can help you stay connected to the financial position of the company.<br /><br />During that review, look at:<br /><br />Current available cash<br /><br />Customer payments received<br /><br />Outstanding invoices<br /><br />Upcoming bills<br /><br />Major expenses<br /><br />Expected revenue<br /><br />Changes in spending<br /><br />This habit can make a major difference over time because it gives you a clearer picture of what is happening inside the business.<br /><br />Make Cash Flow Part of Your Growth Strategy<br /><br />Business growth requires more than generating additional sales.<br /><br />More customers can lead to more invoices. More sales can lead to higher inventory costs. A larger team can increase payroll expenses. New services and products can require upfront investment.<br /><br />Cash flow connects all of these decisions.<br /><br />A business that understands its financial position can plan growth with greater confidence.<br /><br />This is why many companies gradually build more structured financial systems as they expand. Better tracking, forecasting, payment management, and financial software can all help create a clearer picture of the business.<br /><br />The Bottom Line<br /><br />Managing cash flow is about knowing what money is available today and understanding how upcoming payments and expenses will affect the business tomorrow.<br /><br />Start by tracking the money moving through the business. Monitor customer invoices. Plan supplier payments. Review expenses. Build a cash reserve. Create regular forecasts.<br /><br />As your operations become larger and more complex, tools such as BILL can also help organize important parts of your financial workflow.<br /><br />A clear cash flow system gives a growing business something extremely valuable.<br /><br />The ability to make financial decisions with confidence.<br /><br />Frequently Asked Questions<br /><br />What is the easiest way to manage business cash flow?<br /><br />Start by regularly tracking incoming payments, upcoming expenses, outstanding invoices, and available cash. A simple forecast can help you understand how these numbers may change over the coming weeks and months.<br /><br />How often should a business review its cash flow?<br /><br />Many small and growing businesses benefit from reviewing cash flow weekly. Companies with a higher volume of financial transactions may review their financial position more frequently.<br /><br />Can financial software help manage cash flow?<br /><br />Financial software can help businesses organize invoices, payments, expenses, approvals, and financial records. The most suitable platform depends on the size of the business and the complexity of its financial operations.<br /><br />What should a cash flow forecast include?<br /><br />A cash flow forecast can include current available cash, expected customer payments, payroll, supplier invoices, recurring expenses, taxes, loan payments, and planned investments.<p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><br /></div><br /><p></p>
